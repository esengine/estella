**The hierarchy is a relationship the engine stores itself, and world transforms recompose only what moved.** Each entity's parent, its first and last child, its siblings and its depth are kept in flat arrays indexed by entity, in the manner of Flecs' (ChildOf, parent) pairs: setting a parent, detaching, and reordering children cost the same however many children there are, and allocate nothing. World transforms compose by walking every tree as one array in which each parent comes before its children, with no stack and no per-entity lookups of a child list: 100,000 entities in 1,000 trees compose in 2.6 ms where they took 3.4 ms. Removing a child no longer reorders its siblings. The `Parent` and `Children` components are still there for scripts and scene files, kept in step with the relation; the relation is what the engine reads, so a `Parent` written into a component without going through the hierarchy's own edit links nothing. World transforms are then recomposed only where something was written since the last composition: an entity with no parent or children is copied only when written, and a written entity in a tree recomposes its own subtree and nothing else, so 1% of 100,000 entities moving composes in 50 to 80 µs, where it took 0.9 ms in a flat scene and 3.4 ms in a scene of trees. Every engine writer of a transform stamps what it writes (the UI layout and spring bones now do too, and compiled systems stamp whether or not a script watches); a producer that writes the bytes and only calls `invalidateTransformComposition()` makes the next composition compose everything, as before. Moving a subtree to another parent recomposes that subtree and rebuilds only the trees it left and joined: in a world of 100,000 entities, a 100-entity subtree changes parent in 6 µs, where it took 3.5 ms. Destroying a parent now also marks its children's world transforms stale, so they are recomposed as the roots they have become.
