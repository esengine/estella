**A crowded server can cap what each player is sent, with `server.setBandwidthBudget({ bytesPerSecond })`.** Interest management decides whether a player sees an entity; in a town square they see hundreds, and every one of them every tick was more than a phone's link or the server could carry. With a budget, each connection is sent at most its allowance of state updates a second, most urgent first: an entity's priority (by default, how close it is to what the player owns; or the game's own `priority`) adds up while it waits, so distant ones update less often but never stop. A deferred entity is late, not wrong — its next update carries every field that changed since its last one, and the client interpolates across the gap. What a player owns, and spawns, despawns and component removals, always go out. `setBandwidthBudget(null)` lifts it and delivers what was still owed. With 200 players in one crowd at 20 Hz, each player's stream fell from about 52 KB/s to 17.6 KB/s and the server's from 10 MB/s to 3.4 MB/s at the same tick cost; `tools/net-capacity-benchmark.mjs --budget` measures it, and `tools/net-sample-microbench.mjs` times the replication sample alone.
