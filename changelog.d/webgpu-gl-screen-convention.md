section: Changed
commits:
  - feat(webgpu): WebGPU draws with GL's screen convention, so a shader's GLSL means the same on both backends - every WebGPU vertex stage turns clip y over and a target's rows land bottom-up as GL lays them; the backbuffer is drawn into a texture of its own and reaches the swapchain turned over once at the frame's end; viewport, scissor and readback rows, the published copy of every render target, textureOriginTopLeft and the y fixes in the hand-written WGSL are gone; every fullscreen pass and the Water fragment ship as GLSL alone
---
**WebGPU and WebGL now see the screen the same way.** WebGPU used to store a render target top-down, so any shader that turned a screen position into a texture coordinate (light shafts, water refraction, screen-space reflections, a material reading `gl_FragCoord` or `dFdy`) needed a second, hand-written WGSL copy with the y flipped, and a GLSL-only shader came out upside down or lit from the wrong side there. WebGPU now lays a target's rows bottom-up as GL does, so the WGSL written from a shader's GLSL as it compiles is right as written. Every built-in post-process effect and the Water surface now ship as GLSL alone (77 hand-written WGSL copies and 28 KB of Water's removed), a render target no longer keeps a flipped copy of itself each frame, and a project's own `.esshader` that reads the screen position or derivatives draws the same on both backends. A frame drawn to the screen pays one copy at its end instead.
