**Spring bones** add secondary motion to a skeleton: hair, tails, scarves and cords lag behind the body, swing and
settle without authored animation. A `SpringBone` on the **root** of a chain makes every entity below it a spring: the
animation still defines where each bone would be, and the chain trails that pose by inertia, falls with gravity and is
pulled back toward it. The root itself is not simulated; it is driven by the animation or the body.

Spring bones are part of the core engine and run in C++, with the same cost on every target, including targets without
a JIT.

## `SpringBone` component

Add a `SpringBone` to the top bone of a chain, for example the first bone of a hair strand (its parent is the body,
its children are the strand). Every entity below it, along every branch, is simulated.

| Field | Type | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `enabled` | boolean | `true` | Off leaves the chain exactly as the animation poses it. |
| `stiffness` | number | `0.1` | Pull of each bone back toward its animated pose per frame at 60 Hz, 0 to 1. 0 hangs free; 1 follows the animation exactly. |
| `drag` | number | `0.5` | Fraction of its velocity a bone loses each frame, 0 to 1. Higher values settle sooner; the default is damped enough not to oscillate. |
| `gravity` | vec3 | `(0, -980, 0)` | World units per second squared; world Y is up. The default assumes 100 units per metre. |
| `wind` | vec3 | `(0, 0, 0)` | Steady wind acceleration on the chain, world units per second squared. Zero is still air. |
| `windGust` | number | `0` | Gust amplitude, 0 to 1. 0 is a steady wind; 1 varies it from calm to twice its strength. Each bone meets a gust slightly after the one above it, so the strand ripples. |
| `windFrequency` | number | `0.5` | Gusts per second. |
| `endLength` | number | `0` | Length of the tip added past each end bone, in world units; an end bone has no child to aim at. 0 leaves end bones unsimulated. |
| `radius` | number | `0` | Thickness of the strand, in world units, kept clear of the colliders. |
| `weight` | number | `1` | Blend from the animation alone (0) to the full simulation (1). |
| `colliders` | entity list | empty | Entities with a `SpringBoneCollider` that the chain is kept out of. |

### `SpringBoneCollider`

A sphere or capsule that chains listing it are kept out of, placed by its entity's transform. Put colliders on the head
and shoulder bones so hair does not pass through the body.

| Field | Type | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `shape` | `SpringBoneShape` | `Sphere` | `Sphere`, or `Capsule` along the entity's local Y axis. |
| `radius` | number | `10` | Radius in world units, before the entity's scale. |
| `height` | number | `20` | Capsule only: distance between the centres of its end spheres, in world units before scale. |
| `offset` | vec3 | `(0, 0, 0)` | Centre of the shape in the entity's local space. |

## Chains from a model

With a model's **Spring Bones** import setting set to **From File Data and Names**, the importer builds chains and
colliders from the data in the file. Sources are read in this order:

1. **`VRMC_springBone`** (VRM 1.0 extension): springs, colliders and collider groups with their values. A spring
   becomes a `SpringBone` on its first joint (that bone is held, the bones below it move). Its stiffness (per second
   in VRM) and gravity power are converted to the engine's per-frame and acceleration units. Springs that name fewer
   than two joints, or that would move bones they do not name, are reported in the import warnings.
2. **Node properties**: `extras.estella.springBone` (`stiffness`, `drag`, `gravity`, `radius`, `endLength`) on a chain
   root and `extras.estella.springCollider` (`shape`, `radius`, `height`, `offset`) on a collider node. A Blender
   custom property reaches the engine this way with exact values. These take precedence over VRM data on the same node.
3. **Names**, for anything the above do not specify:

| Name | Result |
| --- | --- |
| `Hair_<region>_<chain>_00` (with bones below it) | A `SpringBone` on that bone; the strand is the bones below it. |
| `SBC_Sphere_<name>_r<cm>` | A sphere `SpringBoneCollider`; radius in centimetres. |
| `SBC_Capsule_<name>_r<cm>_h<cm>` | A capsule along the node's local +Y; radius and distance between end centres in centimetres. |

- The region (`Ponytail`, `Temple_L`, `Fringe`, `Crest`, `Crown`) selects initial stiffness and drag: a fringe is held
  close, a ponytail swings.
- Every chain is kept out of every collider in the model.
- Sizes are in the model's metres and follow the import scale; gravity is Earth gravity in those units.
- A capsule with zero length, or a chain root with no bones below it, is skipped.
- Name-based chains are a convention; review the result in the Details panel and adjust it there.

## Editor

### Details panel

The `SpringBone` fields are grouped as **Motion** (stiffness, drag, gravity), **Wind** (wind, gust, frequency),
**Chain** (end length, radius, weight) and **Collision** (colliders). The first line reports the number of bones below
the entity and the number of end tips, or, in the warning colour, that the entity has no child bones and simulates
nothing.

### Viewport gizmos

Drawn for the selection only, under the **Spring bones** entry of the Gizmo menu:

| Selection | Drawing |
|---|---|
| Chain root | The bones as the animation poses them as amber lines; a ring at each joint with the width of `radius` (a dot when it is 0); a tip where `endLength` adds one; each listed collider as a wireframe sphere or capsule in the collider green. |
| `SpringBoneCollider` | Its shape, with six square resize handles. On a sphere all six set the radius; on a capsule the four around its middle set the radius and the two on its ends set the height. Handles on the far side are faded. |

A handle drag is one undo step and shows the value beside the pointer; with Preview FX on, the chains follow it live.
Handles size a collider but do not place it; `offset` is typed in Details or set by moving the entity.
### Preview

**View → Preview FX** (on by default in a new editor; the same switch particles and trails use) runs the chains in the
edit viewport: moving the head or changing a field makes the hair swing. While it is on, the simulated chain is drawn
solid over the animated pose, which is drawn dashed. Turning it off ends the simulation and restores the bones as the
scene stores them. The editor's simulation never writes to the scene file.

## Per-frame solve

The animation runs first and writes the bones' local rotations. Then, for each chain:

1. The animated pose is computed down the chain from the root, using the animation's rotations. A bone this system
   rotated in the previous frame and that no animation wrote this frame is read as the animation had it, not as the
   system left it, so a chain never springs toward its own previous result.
2. The chain is stepped toward that pose. Each bone keeps its velocity less drag, falls with gravity, is pulled toward
   its pose, is restored to its length from its parent (parents first, so bones do not stretch), and is pushed out of
   the colliders. The time step is subdivided into steps of at most one 60 Hz frame.
3. Each bone is rotated to aim at its child's new position. **Only rotations are written**; bone positions never
   change, so a bone cannot separate from its parent and skinning needs no further data.

### Resets

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| The root moves by more than a few chain lengths in one frame | The chain is reset to its pose. |
| A stall of half a second or more | The chain is reset to its pose. |
| The component is disabled or removed | The bones return to the animated pose. |
| `SpringBoneRes.reset(root)` | Explicit reset, for example after a teleport. |

`SpringBoneRes` is read with `app.getResource(SpringBoneRes)`.

## Cost

The solver costs a few arithmetic operations per bone. Measured in the editor's play realm with the WebAssembly
engine: **300 chains of six bones (2100 nodes) cost 0.28 ms per update.** The measurement is the `springbone` editor
check; `test_springbone` is the C++ unit test of the solver and system.

## Limits

- A bone with several children aims at its first child; the other children follow as children and aim at their own.
- Scale is taken from the bones' composed scale; non-uniform scale along a chain is not modelled.
- Collision is bone against sphere or capsule only: no strand-to-strand collision, no self-collision, and no collision
  with physics shapes.
- Wind is a per-chain field, not a scene force; there is no scene-wide wind source shared by all chains.
- The importer builds chains from names and file data, not from the mesh shape; a strand with no `Hair_` bone (and no
  VRM or `extras` data) gets no motion.
