title: Spring bones

---

Hair, tails, scarves and cords move after the body does: they lag it, swing, and settle. **Spring bones** add that to a skeleton without an animator
authoring it. A `SpringBone` on the **root** of a chain makes every entity below it a spring: the animation still says where each bone would be, and
the chain trails that pose by inertia, falls with gravity, and is pulled back toward it. The root itself is not simulated; it is what the animation or
the body drives.

It is core, not an optional module, and it runs in C++ in the engine, so it costs the same on every target, including ones with no JIT.

## The component

Put a `SpringBone` on the top bone of a hair strand (its parent bone is the body; its children are the strand). Everything below it, down every branch,
is simulated.

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| `enabled` | Off leaves the chain exactly as the animation poses it. |
| `stiffness` | How hard each bone is pulled back toward its animated pose, 0 to 1 a frame at 60 Hz. 0 hangs free. Default 0.1. |
| `drag` | How much of its speed a bone loses each frame, 0 to 1. Higher settles sooner; the default (0.5) is damped enough not to ring. |
| `gravity` | World units a second squared (world y is up). The default is `{0, -980, 0}`: a metre a hundred units. |
| `wind` | A steady wind on the chain, world units a second squared. Zero is still air. |
| `windGust` | How far gusts swing the wind's strength, 0 to 1. 0 is steady; 1 takes it from calm to twice its strength. Each bone meets a gust a moment after the one above, so the strand ripples. |
| `windFrequency` | Gusts a second (default 0.5). |
| `endLength` | A leaf bone has no child to aim at; this gives it a tip that long. 0 leaves leaf bones unsimulated. |
| `radius` | The thickness of the strand, kept clear of the colliders. |
| `weight` | How much of the simulation is used, 0 to 1 (default 1). 0 is the animation alone. |
| `colliders` | Entities with a `SpringBoneCollider` the chain is kept out of. |

A `SpringBoneCollider` is a sphere (the default, `radius` 10), or a capsule along its entity's own Y axis (`height` 20, the distance between its end centres), placed by the entity's transform (and its `offset`). Put them on the head
and shoulders bones so hair does not pass through the body.

## From a model

Set a model's **Spring Bones** import setting to **From File Data and Names** and the importer builds the chains from what the file already says, so nobody sets up forty strands by hand. It looks, in this order, at:

1. **`VRMC_springBone`**, the VRM 1.0 extension: springs, colliders and collider groups with their numbers. A spring becomes a `SpringBone` on its first joint (that bone is held, the ones below it move), and its stiffness (per second in VRM) and gravity power are converted to the engine's per-frame and acceleration units. Springs that name fewer than two joints, or that would move bones they do not name, are reported in the import warnings.
2. **A node's own properties**, `extras.estella.springBone` (`stiffness`, `drag`, `gravity`, `radius`, `endLength`) on a chain root and `extras.estella.springCollider` (`shape`, `radius`, `height`, `offset`) on a collider node, which is how a Blender custom property reaches the engine with exact sizes. These win over VRM for the node they are on.
3. **Names**, for whatever those leave unsaid:

| Name | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| `Hair_<region>_<chain>_00` (with bones below it) | a `SpringBone` on that bone; the rest of the strand is whatever hangs below it |
| `SBC_Sphere_<name>_r<cm>` | a sphere `SpringBoneCollider`, radius in centimetres |
| `SBC_Capsule_<name>_r<cm>_h<cm>` | a capsule, radius and the distance between its two end centres, along the node's local +Y |

The region (`Ponytail`, `Temple_L`, `Fringe`, `Crest`, `Crown`) picks a starting stiffness and drag: a fringe is held close, a ponytail swings. Every chain is kept out of every collider the model has. Sizes are in the model's metres and follow the import scale, and gravity is Earth's in those metres. A capsule with no length, or a chain root with nothing below it, is skipped. A name is a convention, so look at what the import made in Details and adjust it there.

## In the editor

Details groups the chain's fields into **Motion** (stiffness, drag, gravity), **Chain** (end length, radius, weight) and **Collision** (colliders), and says on its first
line how many bones the chain has below its entity and how many end tips, or, in the warning colour, that it has no child bones and so simulates nothing.

Select a chain's root and the viewport draws it, and the colliders it keeps out of, in the **Spring bones** entry of the Gizmo menu: the bones as the animation poses them
as amber lines, a ring at each joint the width of the strand's `radius` (a dot where it is 0), a tip where `endLength` gives one, and each collider as a wireframe sphere or
capsule in the collider green. Select a `SpringBoneCollider` and its own shape is drawn, with six square handles to size it: on a sphere all six set the radius, on a capsule the four round its middle set the radius and the two on its ends set the height (the distance between the centres of its end spheres). The handles on the far side of the shape are faded. A drag is one undo step, shows the value beside the pointer, and the strands follow it live while Preview FX is on. Nothing is drawn for what is not selected, so a character's hair does not fill the viewport.

**View → Preview FX** (on in a fresh editor, the same switch particles and trails use) runs the chain in the edit viewport: drag the head, or change a field, and the
hair swings. With it on, the simulated chain is drawn solid over the animated pose, dashed, so the difference you are tuning is the difference you see. Turning it off
ends the simulation and gives the bones back as the scene has them. The simulation is the editor's own: it never writes into the scene file.

## What it does each frame

The animation runs first and writes the bones' local rotations. Then, for each chain:

1. The pose the animation gave is worked out down the chain from the root, using the animation's own rotations. (A bone this system turned last frame
   and that nothing animated this frame is read as the animation had it, not as it was left, so a chain never springs toward its own last result.)
2. The chain is stepped toward that pose: each bone keeps its velocity less drag, falls with gravity, is pulled toward its pose, is put back at its length
   from its parent (parents first, so a bone cannot stretch), and is pushed out of the colliders. The time is split into steps of at most a 60 Hz frame.
3. Each bone is turned to aim at where its child has got to. **Only rotations are written**; a bone's position is never changed, so it cannot leave its parent,
   and skinning needs nothing else.

A jump of the root by more than a few times the chain's length, or a stall of half a second, puts the chain back at its pose instead of whipping it.
Switching the component off, or removing it, gives the bones back as the animation had them. `SpringBoneRes` (`app.getResource(SpringBoneRes)`) has
`reset(root)` for a teleport you want to mark yourself.

## Cost

The solver is a few arithmetic operations per bone. Measured in the editor's play realm, in the wasm engine: **three hundred chains of six bones (2100 nodes)
cost 0.28 ms an update.** The test is `springbone` in the editor checks; `test_springbone` is the C++ unit test of the solver and the system.

## Limits

- A bone with several children aims at its first child; the others follow by being children, and aim at their own.
- Scale is taken from the bones' composed scale; non-uniform scale down a chain is not modelled.
- Collision is bone against sphere or capsule: no collision between strands, no self-collision, and no collision with the world's physics shapes.
- The wind is a field on each chain, not a force in the scene: there is no scene-wide wind source to set once for every chain. A collider's offset is typed in Details, or set by moving its entity; the handles size it, they do not place it. Chains are made from names; the importer does not look at
  the shape of the mesh, so a strand with no `Hair_` bone gets no motion.
