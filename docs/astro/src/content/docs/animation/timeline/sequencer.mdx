The **Sequencer** edits `.estimeline` assets. It opens when a timeline is created with **Add → New Animation**, when
an `.estimeline` is double-clicked, or from **Panels → Sequencer** on the left toolbar.

## Key a property

The following procedure moves a sprite such as `Hero` along X from its current position to 120 in one second. The
same procedure applies to a 3D object.

<Steps>

1. **Create the timeline.** Open the destination folder in the Content Browser and choose **Add → New Animation**.
   The editor creates `NewAnimation.estimeline` and opens it in the Sequencer. In an empty Sequencer panel,
   **New animation** does the same.

   ![The New animation button in an empty Sequencer](@/assets/guides/en/seq-first-new.png)

2. **Bind the entity and add a channel.** Select the entity in the **World Outliner**. The link button at the upper
   left of the Sequencer shows its name; if it reads **Unbound**, click it. Click **Jump to start** to place the
   playhead at frame 0. Open **+ Track**, scroll to **Property (from the bound entity)** and choose `position.x`
   under `Transform`. The Sheet adds the channel with a first key at the entity's current X position.

   ![The property section of the Track menu with position.x highlighted for Transform](@/assets/guides/en/seq-first-track.png)

3. **Add the end key.** Click the ruler at **1.00 s** (frame 12 at the default 12 fps). Hover over the `position.x`
   row and click its diamond **Key at playhead** button. Click the new key in the lane, enter `120` in
   **Details → Value** and press Enter. The Sheet footer shows **2 keys**.

   ![The second key is selected at frame 12, its value is 120 in Details, and the Sheet counts two keys](@/assets/guides/en/seq-first-keys.png)

4. **Preview and save.** Drag the playhead between frame 0 and frame 12, or click the play triangle, to see the
   entity move in the viewport. Click **Save** at the upper right of the Sequencer to write the `.estimeline`.

</Steps>

To play the timeline in the game, add **Timeline Player** to the same entity (**Details → Add Component**, search
`TimelinePlayer`), assign the saved `.estimeline` to **Timeline**, tick **Playing**, save the scene and press Play.
When the clip previews in the Sequencer but does not play in the game, check which entity has the player and which
file it references.

### Recording

With the entity bound, move the playhead to a time and click the red record button next to the link button. Moving
the entity with the Move tool, or editing its fields in **Details**, keys the **existing property channels of the
bound entity** at the playhead. Click record again to stop. Recording also changes the entity's scene values; editing
a key's value in **Details** leaves the scene values unchanged.

## Audio items

1. Place the playhead where the sound starts.
2. Choose **+ Track → Audio** and pick **(timeline root)** or a descendant. The Sequencer creates and selects an
   audio item at the playhead; **No sound** indicates that no file is assigned. If Details shows track settings,
   click the item's diamond in the lane.
3. In **Details → Audio → Sound**, click **None ▾** and choose the audio file. Click **Save**.

   ![The Sound picker listing the project files Bell.wav and Intro.mp3](@/assets/guides/en/seq-sound-picker.png)

An item with **Length** `0` is a one-shot: the whole sound plays once when a playing timeline crosses it, which is
heard when the scene runs. An item with a positive **Length** is a clip drawn as a bar and heard in the Sequencer
during playback ([Audio tracks](/docs/animation/timeline/tracks/#audio-tracks)).

The target submenu of **+ Track** can be operated from the keyboard: ↑ and ↓ highlight an entry, → opens it, and
Enter picks the entity.

## Sheet and Curves

The `cutscene` example's `assets/timelines/intro.estimeline` shows both views. The **Sheet** lists tracks and current
values on the left and a frame ruler on the right; dragging the playhead along the ruler scrubs the timeline. Key
shapes indicate interpolation modes (circles and diamonds on the `Hero` position channels). Activation ranges are
drawn as bars (the two letterbox ranges below).

![The cutscene Sheet with the Hero position keys above two three-second letterbox activation ranges](@/assets/guides/en/seq-dopesheet.png)

**Curves** shows each channel as a curve. Selecting a key shows its two tangent handles. Dragging a key changes its
time and value; dragging a handle changes the slope at that key.

![A selected key on the character's height curve with its frame, value and two tangent handles](@/assets/guides/en/seq-curves.png)

## Zoom and markers

**Ctrl+wheel** over the lanes zooms the time axis about the pointer, keeping the time under the pointer fixed; the
ruler shows finer ticks as it zooms. When zoomed, the lanes scroll horizontally and the view follows the playhead
during playback. **Fit** shows the whole timeline. Every marker of a marker track is drawn on the ruler by name;
clicking it moves the playhead to the marker.

## Selection and editing

Dragging over empty lanes draws a selection box; the keys and clips it touches are selected. **Shift** or **Ctrl**
adds to the selection, also when clicking a single item. Dragging any selected item moves all selected keys and
clips across tracks as one undo step; items on locked tracks do not move. Clicking one item of a selection selects it
alone.

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **Ctrl+C** / **Ctrl+X** | Copy / cut the selection. |
| **Ctrl+V** | Paste at the playhead, preserving layout, into the tracks and channels the items came from. |
| **Ctrl+D** | Duplicate the selection immediately after itself. |
| **S** | Split each selected clip that contains the playhead. The second part continues where the first stops (an animation's or sound's Clip In advances by the time cut off); the cut is a hard edge with no ease or fade. |
| **Delete** | Delete the selection. |
| **Ctrl+A** | Select every key and clip. |

A clip's context menu also provides Split, Copy and Paste.

## Entity tracks

Open **+ Track**, choose an entity track type such as Audio, Active, Animation, Spawn or Camera shake, and pick
**(timeline root)** or a descendant. The first item is placed at the playhead. Drag a clip's body to move it or an
edge to change its duration; a particle action, marker or event at a single instant is dragged along the lane. For
tracks that hold several items, the row's **+** adds another item at the playhead. Item fields (sound asset, volume,
animation, event data) are edited in **Details**.

**+ Track → Fade** and **+ Track → Time dilation** add the `ScreenFade` or `TimeDilation` component to the root if it
has none, and a track with a key at the playhead.

## Mute, lock and folders

Each track row has a **mute** toggle (the eye) and a **lock**; both are saved with the timeline.

- A muted track or muted property channel is excluded wherever the timeline plays: in the game, in an Animator state
  that plays it, in a nested sequence and in the Sequencer preview.
- A locked track cannot be edited until unlocked; its items can still be selected and the track can still be muted.
- A track's context menu provides mute, lock, **Move to group** (into an existing or a new folder), rename and
  delete. Folders collapse like any row. Double-click a folder or track name to rename it.

In the file these are `muted`, `locked` and `group` on a track and `muted` on a channel, written only when set.

![The Sequencer open on intro.estimeline in the cutscene example: the Hero transform tracks with their keys, the BarTop activation track, and the Director's Timeline Player card in Details](@/assets/guides/en/anim-seq-track-menu.png)
## Preview root

The link button names the **preview root**: the entity whose name paths the tracks resolve against. Tracks can
animate the root, its descendants, and other scene entities bound through the player
([Bindings](/docs/animation/timeline/tracks/#bindings)).

When a timeline opens, the Sequencer looks for a `TimelinePlayer` that plays the file on the selected entity or its
ancestors, then for the first matching player in the scene. With no matching player it uses the current selection.
When the button reads **Unbound**, select the target in the World Outliner and click the link button.

Scrubbing shows property-channel values in the scene viewport, the camera shot in the viewport's camera preview, a
fade over the scene view, camera shakes, animation-track blends and spawned instances. The preview does not change
the scene's own values. Activation ranges, sprite clip starts and one-shot sounds are checked by running the scene;
a slower clock from time dilation is visible only in Play.

## Render to frames or a video

**Render** in the Sequencer header renders the timeline to images. The dialog sets the frame rate (24, 30 or 60), the
format (**MP4**, **WebM** or **PNG frames**) and the range (**From**, **To**).

**Render** captures the open clip, including unsaved keys. The preview root must have a `TimelinePlayer` bound to
that clip. A separate game instance renders its canvas at **From**, then advances one frame at the chosen rate
for each subsequent PNG. You can render while your Play session is running or paused.

Each render creates its own folder under `renders/`, with files starting at `frame_00000.png`. For video formats the
bundled ffmpeg writes an MP4 or WebM beside that folder. **Cancel** keeps the frames already written.

## Editing through an agent

An agent driving the editor over [MCP](/docs/agents/mcp/) edits timelines with the Sequencer's own commands in the
`timeline` tool group:

| Tool | Effect |
|---|---|
| `timeline_add_track` | Adds a track of any type on an entity, named by id or by its name path below the player. |
| `timeline_add_items` | Adds shots, clips, sounds, spawns, markers and events, with fields named as the file stores them. |
| `timeline_set_keys` | Keys an animatable field, creating its property track. |
| `save_asset_document` | Writes the file. |

Each call is one undo step. An invalid name (a field the track does not have, an entity outside the player, a field
that cannot be animated) is refused with the valid names.
