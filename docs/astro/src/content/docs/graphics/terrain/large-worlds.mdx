This page covers two forms of large ground built on the [Terrain](/docs/graphics/terrain/overview/) machinery: tiled heightmaps
for very large areas, and `Planet`, ground on a sphere.

## Tiled ground

A heightmap is read whole: an 8193 × 8193 field occupies about 256 MiB. Set the terrain's `tiles` to a `.esterrain` file
and the ground is stored as tiles of 513 × 513 samples (neighbours share their edge row) plus a low-detail overview.

- Distant ground is drawn from the overview, which is always resident.
- Full-detail tiles are read only near the view or a moving body. Beyond a 256 MiB budget the least recently used tiles
  are released. Where no tile has been read the height is unknown, and collision and queries wait for it.
- **Split into Tiles** in the terrain sculpt panel converts a heightmap terrain to tiles.
- Tiled ground is sculpted and painted like any other, on the resident tiles: a brush dab over a tile not yet read is
  skipped and the tile is read. A modified tile stays resident until saved; saving writes only the modified tiles and the
  overview.
- The sculpt panel exports the heights as one image the size of the area and imports one over the tiles. With
  **Loaded tiles**, non-resident tiles are not read: export takes them from the overview, and import writes the resident
  tiles and the overview only. **All tiles** reads and writes every tile. Import is refused while there are unsaved
  changes.
- Tile images are referenced only by the `.esterrain` and, like a `heightmap`, are read as data; none is uploaded as a
  texture.

Measured in the editor on a 4096-unit area: an 8193 × 8193 field opened as tiles holds 36 MiB, and 45 MiB close to the
ground.

## Planet

`Planet` is ground on a sphere. It uses the same machinery as flat terrain over a cube whose six faces are each a
quadtree, with every point projected out to the radius. The finest cells are under the eye; a planet seen from far away
is a handful of coarse nodes. The same ground is drawn from the surface and from space with no transition.

### Fields

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `radius` | Radius in metres. The planet stands at its entity, unrotated and unscaled. |
| `provider`, `world`, `generatorVersion`, `seed` | Generator (registered with `registerPlanetProvider`), world name, version and seed. The same four produce the same ground everywhere. A version the provider no longer lists fails the planet. |
| `cellSize`, `detail` | Finest mesh cell in metres, and how far the finest cells reach (above 1 is further). |
| `layerColor0`–`3` | Grass, dirt, snow and rock colours. |
| `macroScale`, `macroVariation` | Size in metres and strength (0–1) of a brightness variation laid over the layers. |
| `layers` | Up to four `.esterrainlayer` files. Empty draws the four colours. |
| `tintLayers` | Multiply the layer textures by the four layer colours, for grey grain textures that take each planet's colours. Off draws layers in their own colours. |
| `collision`, `collisionDistance`, `collisionLayer`, `friction` | As on flat terrain: whether bodies collide, the build radius round each moving body (metres), the layer, and friction. |
| `gravity` | Pull toward the centre at the surface, in m/s² (9.81 is Earth's). 0 (default) pulls nothing. Requires 3D physics. |
| `sea`, `seaLevel`, `seaColor`, `seaOpacity` | Whether the planet has a sea, its height above the radius (metres), its colour, and its opacity (0–1). |
| `seaWaveLength`, `seaWaveSteepness` | Longest wave, crest to crest, in metres (shorter trains ride on it), and wave steepness (0 is a mirror). |
| `seaShallowDepth` | Depth in metres at which the bottom is lost to the water colour. Shallower water is clearer and paler and breaks white along the shore. |

The quality level's **Planet draw levels** (12 at High, 11 at Medium, 10 at Low) caps how many tree levels are drawn
below a whole face. Collision and queries keep the authored `cellSize`.

### Providers

A planet provider answers for **directions**, not positions. It receives unit directions from the planet's centre and
the radius, and returns each direction's height above the radius and optionally four layer weights. A sphere has no x
and z, so a generator samples three-dimensional noise at the point, which has no seam and no pole.

```typescript
import { registerPlanetProvider } from 'esengine/terrain';

registerPlanetProvider({
    id: 'my-game:planet',
    versions: [1],
    range: () => [-40, 200],
    sample({ seed, radius, directions, count }) {
        const heights = new Float32Array(count);
        for (let i = 0; i < count; i++) heights[i] = myHeight(seed, radius, directions[i * 3]!, directions[i * 3 + 1]!, directions[i * 3 + 2]!);
        return { heights };
    },
});
```

A provider may also set `workerSource`: the text of a self-contained plain-JavaScript module whose default export
provides `sample`, `fields`, `versions` and `id`. The world is then laid out and the ground painted on worker threads,
and the main thread only places each result. Without it, the work runs on the main thread.

### Queries and gravity

`PlanetRegistryRes` answers the questions a round world poses:

| Method | Returns |
|---|---|
| `nearest(x, y, z)` | The planet whose surface is nearest the point. |
| `up(planet, x, y, z)` | Unit direction away from the planet's centre. |
| `altitude(planet, x, y, z)` | Height above the radius. |
| `groundHeight(planet, x, y, z)` | The generator's height under the position (`null` while an asynchronous provider has not answered). |
| `groundPoint(planet, x, y, z)` | The point on the ground under the position. |

With `gravity` set, 3D physics pulls every dynamic body toward the centre of the nearest planet that has gravity: the
world gravity is removed from the body and the planet's applied as a force each fixed step. With world gravity at zero,
a body falls toward the planet.

### Generated worlds on a planet

Place a `WorldGen` with `surface` set to **Planet** on the `Planet`'s entity to lay out a
[generated world](/docs/world/generation/) over the sphere. Each cube face is laid out as a plane in metres by the same
rules as a flat world (biomes over fields, recipes, layers); a direction is planned by the face it falls on, and placed
content stands along the planet's up.

- Available fields: ground height, slope, three-dimensional noise (seamless across face edges), `paint.<layer>` for each
  planet layer, and fields the provider declares in `fields`.
- Biomes paint the ground (`paintGround`), so what is drawn and what grows come from one map.
- `seaLevel` is the sea height; lower ground is under water.
- `WorldZone`s stand at a world position and are kept on the face they fall on.

### Collision and layers

The finest nodes within `collisionDistance` of each moving 3D body and character are built as collision geometry from the
samples the ground is drawn from. A planet's transform is a position only: collision of a rotated or scaled planet stands
where the unrotated planet would be.

Each layer is projected onto the planes of the world axes by how much the ground faces them, with a wider tile mixed in
at a distance. Layers are drawn by the same code as the Terrain Layers shader, so a layer's mask darkens sky light on a
planet as on flat ground (including its `occlusion`). The Planet material has the same `u_heightBlend`, `u_blendNoise`
and `u_antiTile`; they default to 0.

### Sea and sky

With `sea` on, the planet has a translucent, lit sphere at `seaLevel` above the radius, in which lower ground stands. The
sea carries trains of waves laid over the sphere as surface slope, is clearer and paler over shallow ground, and breaks
white along the shore.

The sky follows the planet's curvature: give `skyAtmosphere` and `heightFog` the planet's radius and centre, and they
compute sky and haze for the ground under the camera. The sun is a fixed direction, so day and night depend on where on
the planet the camera stands.

## Limits

- **The planet sea does not move the geometry.** Its waves are surface slope only; the sphere mesh is too coarse to carry
  them. The flat [water](/docs/graphics/water/overview/) system's displacement, ripples and caustics do not apply to it.
- **A planet's transform is a position.** Rotation and scale are ignored.
- **Tiles not yet read have no height.** Queries and collision over them wait until the tile is loaded.
