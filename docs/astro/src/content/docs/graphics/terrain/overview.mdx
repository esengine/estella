`Terrain` draws heightfield ground as a quadtree of identical patches centred on the eye: cells are finest near the
camera and double in size level by level with distance. The ground is a height for each (x, z) position; caves,
overhangs and holes require [Voxel Terrain](/docs/world/voxel-terrain/).

Terrain is an optional module (`esengine/terrain`). A package includes it when a scene contains a `Terrain` or a script
imports the module.

## Component

Add `Terrain` to an entity. The ground is positioned at the entity; its children are the quadtree nodes, created at run
time and in the editor's scene view and updated as the eye moves. Only the component is saved; no geometry is baked into
the scene.

`extent` selects one of two kinds of ground:

| `extent` | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Area** | Ground `sizeX` × `sizeZ`, centred on the entity, from a heightmap, a tile set, or (with neither) a window onto its provider's ground at the area's world position. Two noise areas of the same size placed side by side join; moving an area moves the window, not the ground. Supports erosion, splatmaps, a colour map and an occlusion map. |
| **Infinite** | Ground produced by a provider at any world position, without an edge, drawn round the eye out to `drawDistance`. The same provider, world, seed and version produce the same ground in the editor, in a game and on a server. |

### Shape

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `extent` | `Area` or `Infinite`. |
| `heightmap` | Area only. An image read as data: the file's bytes with no colour conversion and no GPU texture, shipped as the PNG it is. A heightmap compressed during export fails the terrain with a message. Assigning an image here marks its texture **Data** in its import settings (see [Textures](/docs/assets/textures/)); no size cap applies unless one is set, and a capped image keeps its 16 bits. Empty generates the ground from noise. |
| `tiles` | Area only. A `.esterrain` file that replaces `heightmap`: tiles read near the view and a coarse overview for the rest. See [Tiled ground](/docs/graphics/terrain/large-worlds/#tiled-ground). |
| `heightmapFormat` | **Grey**: the grey (or red) channel at the file's bit depth — 16 bits from a 16-bit PNG, 256 levels from an 8-bit PNG. **Red + green**: 16 bits split across red (high byte) and green (low byte) of an 8-bit image. |
| `sizeX`, `sizeZ` | Area size in world units. For infinite ground `sizeX` is the span over which `frequency` hills are spread, and `sizeZ` is unused. |
| `height` | World height of the highest ground. |
| `provider`, `world`, `generatorVersion` | Infinite ground, or an area with no heightmap: the generator (`estella:noise`, the engine's noise, or one registered by the game), the world name passed to it, and the generator version the world was made with. A version the provider no longer lists fails the terrain. |
| `erosion` | 0–4. Rain erosion strength; see [Erosion](#erosion). 0 leaves the ground unchanged. |

### Noise

Used when the ground comes from `estella:noise`.

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `seed` | Noise seed. A different seed produces different ground. |
| `frequency` | Number of hills across the area (or across `sizeX` for infinite ground). |
| `octaves` | Layers of detail, 1–8. |
| `persistence` | Height kept by each finer octave, 0–1. Lower is smoother. |
| `ridged` | 0 produces rolling hills; 1 produces sharp ridges. |
| `resolution` | Area only. Samples along each side of the noise field (17–2049). |
| `warp` | 0–1. Domain warp of the noise by noise of its own: winding valleys and bent ridges instead of round hills. |

### Layers

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `layers` | Up to eight [terrain layer](#terrain-layers) files, in the order the paint weighs them. With layers, the ground uses the **Terrain Layers** shader. Empty draws the four layer colours, or the textures of a material on the Terrain shader. |
| `material` | A material on the **Terrain** shader, or on **Terrain Layers** when `layers` is set. Empty uses the stock material. |
| `splatmap` | Area only. RGBA weights of layers 1–4. Empty paints by height and slope. |
| `splatmap2` | Area only. RGBA weights of layers 5–8, read together with `splatmap`. |
| `colorMap` | Area only. A texture laid over the whole area and mixed into the ground colour by its alpha (flower meadows, map regions, scorched ground). Import **Use**: Colour. Its first row lies along the area's minimum z, as the heightmap's does. |
| `colorMapStrength` | 0–1. Colour-map contribution where its alpha is 1. Read every frame; changing it rebuilds nothing. |
| `occlusionMap` | Area only. A texture whose red channel is the share of sky light reaching the ground: baked occlusion for gullies, hollows and cliff feet, which are too large for screen-space occlusion. It dims ambient light only; direct sunlight is left to shadows. Import **Use**: Data. |
| `occlusionStrength` | 0–1. Amount of the occlusion map applied. |
| `rockSlope` | Slope at which ground turns to rock (0 flat, 1 sheer). |
| `dirtHeight`, `snowHeight` | Heights, as a share of `height`, where grass gives way to dirt and where snow begins. |
| `layerColor0`–`3` | Grass, dirt, snow, rock colours used when the ground has no material. |

### Detail

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `cellSize` | The finest mesh cell, in world units, used near the eye. Cells double level by level with distance. |
| `detail` | 0.25–8. Multiplier on how far the finest cells reach. Above 1 is finer at a distance and costs more triangles. |
| `drawDistance` | Infinite only. Distance from the eye to which the ground is drawn. |
| `virtualTexture` | Bake the layered surface into pages; see [Virtual texture](/docs/graphics/terrain/rendering-and-code/#virtual-texture). Ground with layers only. |
| `virtualTextureFrom` | First quadtree level drawn from baked pages: 0 includes the nearest ground, 1 (default) bakes all levels but the nearest. |

### Collision

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `collision` | Whether 3D bodies and characters collide with the ground. |
| `collisionDistance` | Radius, in world units, round each moving body and character within which collision tiles are built. |
| `collisionLayer` | Advanced. Collision layer, 0–15. |
| `friction` | Advanced. Ground friction. |

Changing `detail`, `drawDistance` or a layer colour takes effect on the next frame. Changing any other field rebuilds
the ground.

### Erosion

On an area, `erosion` simulates hydraulic erosion: drops of rain run downhill over the field, cut the ground where they
can carry more sediment and deposit it where they slow. The result is gullies on slopes, sharper ridges between them
and silt in valleys. At 1 there is one drop per field sample (capped at 600 000 drops). The same seed produces the same
result. Erosion applies to heightmap fields as well as noise fields. Grass is replaced with dirt along the channels the
water ran. Erosion of a large field runs over several frames and is shown in progress (see
[Building over frames](/docs/graphics/terrain/rendering-and-code/#building-over-frames)).

Infinite ground has no edge for water to drain off, so its erosion is applied in the noise: each octave is damped where
the octaves below it are already steep. This produces smooth valleys between sharp ridges at no cost beyond the noise.
It paints no dirt channels.

## Terrain layers

A terrain layer (`.esterrainlayer`; **New → New Terrain Layer** in the editor) describes one ground material — grass,
path dirt, sand, rock — as a set of textures. A `Terrain` lists up to eight layers in `layers`; the splatmaps weigh them
(first splatmap RGBA: layers 1–4; second: layers 5–8). Without splatmaps the first four are painted by height and slope
as grass, dirt, snow and rock, and a [generated world](/docs/world/generation/)'s biomes can paint all eight.

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `albedo` | Colour texture, sRGB. Empty is white, coloured by `tint`. |
| `normal` | Tangent-space normal map, green up (OpenGL convention). Empty is flat. |
| `mask` | Red: ambient occlusion. Green: roughness. Blue: height. Empty: no occlusion, `roughness`, and albedo brightness as height. |
| `tile` | World units covered by one texture repeat (default 400, i.e. 4 m at 100 units per metre). |
| `tint` | RGB multiplier on the albedo. |
| `roughness` | Roughness where there is no mask (default 0.9). |
| `normalStrength` | Normal-map strength: 1 as authored, 0 flat. |
| `heightBlend` | Optional, 0–1. How much the layer's own height decides where it meets another: 0 blends by paint alone (grass into sand), 1 by layer heights (stones standing through grass). Where several layers meet, each counts by its weight. Absent: the material's `u_heightBlend`. |
| `occlusion` | Optional, 0–1. Share of sky light reaching this layer, multiplied by the mask's occlusion. Lets vegetated ground take less sky light than bare rock. Direct lights are not dimmed. Absent: 1. |

The **Terrain Layers** shader (`builtin:terrain-layers`) draws layered ground:

- Every layer is sampled by world position with no visible repeat near the eye, and with a wider tile mixed in at a
  distance.
- Where layers meet, the layer whose mask height is higher shows (stones through grass, sand between stones).
- On slopes each layer is also projected from the sides (triplanar), so cliffs are not stretched.
- Normal maps and mask occlusion and roughness contribute to lighting.
- Each point is drawn from the four layers with the most weight there; further layers would be a sliver of the blend
  after sharpening and would cost every ground pixel the texture reads of all eight.

| Parameter | Description |
|---|---|
| `u_sharpness`, `u_heightBlend`, `u_blendNoise`, `u_antiTile`, `u_macroVariation`, `u_wetLevel`, `u_wetBand` | As on the [Terrain material](#terrain-material). |
| `u_farScale` | Scale of the wider tile mixed in at a distance. |
| `u_triplanarFrom` | Slope at which side projection begins. |
| `u_strataWarp`, `u_strataSpan` | Side-projected layers are read by height, so a cliff texture's beds lie level. `u_strataWarp` is how far the beds wander up and down, by slow noise `u_strataSpan` wide (0, the default, keeps them straight). |
| `u_antiTileTurn` | How far a cell of the no-repeat sampling may be rotated. A layer with directional grain (rippled sand) shows the hexagonal cells when rotated; 0 offsets cells without rotating them. |
| `u_surfaceUp` | Steepest ground (as the up component of the normal, default 0.65, about 50°) drawn from the virtual texture; steeper ground is shaded live. |

**Texture requirements.** Per layer: three square, power-of-two, seamlessly tiling textures. Layers are packed into
texture arrays at the size of the largest layer, at most 1024 (512 on minigame platforms), so 1024 is sufficient. Import
the normal map and the mask as data (not colour-managed). Layer textures are packed when the terrain is built and again
whenever a layer file or one of its textures is saved.

## Terrain material

Without layer files the ground has four fixed layers — grass, dirt, snow and rock — drawn by the **Terrain** shader
(`builtin:terrain`). Each point carries a weight for each layer, from the splatmap or from height and slope. Textures are
tiled by world position, so a layer runs unbroken across the ground, and rock is projected from three sides.

| Parameter | Description |
|---|---|
| `u_layer0`–`3` | Layer textures: grass, dirt, snow, rock. |
| `u_tint0`–`3` | Per-layer colour multiplier. |
| `u_tile0`–`3` | World units covered by one repeat of each layer. |
| `u_sharpness` | Dominance of the heavier layer at a seam: 1 is a linear blend, higher is a harder edge. |
| `u_heightBlend` | How much seams follow layer relief (texture brightness): rock shows through gaps in grass before the grass gives way; sand fills the low places between stones. 0 is a plain blend. |
| `u_antiTile` | Amount of each layer re-sampled rotated on a wider tile and blended in by slow noise, hiding repeats across a field. Fades out with distance. |
| `u_macroVariation` | Wide, slow brightness variation over all layers. |
| `u_relief` | Depth, in world units, at which layer relief (brightness as height) affects lighting. 0 is smooth. |
| `u_wetLevel`, `u_wetBand` | Wet ground at a waterline: below `u_wetLevel + u_wetBand` (world heights) the ground darkens and becomes glossier, most at `u_wetLevel`. A band of 0 disables it. |
| `u_roughness` | Roughness of dry ground. |

Other parameters (`u_heightPages`, `u_lodRange0` and similar) are written by the `Terrain` component every frame and are
not authored in the material.

## Ground blending

Three mechanisms hide the line where an object meets the ground. None requires the ground to be a terrain.

### Ground Blend material

The `builtin:ground-blend` shader is the [Model](/docs/graphics/materials/overview/#built-in-shader-templates) shader, with all its maps and parameters, for
rocks, kerbs and foundations. Where the surface comes close to what is rendered behind it, it fades into that pixel of the
scene, so the seam blends into the ground as drawn (its layers, lighting and shadows), with nothing baked.

| Parameter | Description |
|---|---|
| `u_blendDistance` | Height above the surface behind at which the material is fully opaque, in world units. Measured as a height, not along the view, so the band holds as the camera tilts. 0 is a hard edge. |
| `u_blendNoise`, `u_blendNoiseScale` | Edge irregularity, and its world-space scale. |
| `u_layer2`, `u_layer2Tint`, `u_layer2Roughness` | A second layer: dirt, moss, wet stone. |
| `u_layer2Tile` | World length of one repeat of the second layer, projected from above so it lines up across objects. 0 uses the mesh's own UV. |
| `u_layerReach` | Height up from the surface behind to which the second layer climbs. |
| `u_vertexBlend` | 1: vertex-colour red paints the second layer, and vertex colour no longer tints the surface. |

A material on this shader is drawn after a copy of the opaque scene is taken, as water is; a scene containing one takes
that copy even with no post-process effect of its own.

### `RoadDecal`

`RoadDecal` on an entity with a `Spline` lays the entity's `MeshRenderer` along the curve and over the ground, row by
row. It is rebuilt when the spline or the ground changes. With no terrain it lies along the curve as authored. Each edge
fades over `edgeFade`, so use a blended material.

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `step` | Row spacing along the curve, in world units. Smaller follows the ground and bends more closely. |
| `across` | Cells across the road (1–32). More follows a crowned or sloping ground. |
| `widthScale` | Multiplier on the width stored at each spline point. |
| `lift` | Height above the ground. Distant ground is drawn coarser than requested, so a road seen from far needs more lift. |
| `edgeFade` | Share of the half width over which each edge fades. |
| `tile` | Length of one texture repeat along the curve. |

### Snow and wet surfaces

The `snowCover` [post-process effect](/docs/graphics/post-processing/sky-and-weather/#snow-cover) lays snow on any surface that faces the sky and is
not covered, terrain and objects alike, without changing materials. Parameters: `u_amount`, `u_snowLine` and
`u_snowLineBand` (the height it stops at), `u_slopeLimit` and `u_grainScale`. The `Snow` [weather](/docs/graphics/weather/)
adds it. The `rain` effect with `u_intensity` at 0 and `u_wetness` raised produces wet ground without falling rain.

## Limits

- **One height per position.** Caves, overhangs and holes require [Voxel Terrain](/docs/world/voxel-terrain/), or meshes
  placed with the terrain.
- **Position only.** The terrain follows its entity's position; rotation and scale are ignored by `heightAt` and by the
  shader. Keep the entity unrotated and unscaled, and size the ground with `sizeX`, `sizeZ` and `height`.
- **Infinite ground comes from its provider.** It takes no heightmap or splatmap, and its erosion paints no dirt.
- **Paint resolution equals page resolution.** Layer weights are sampled with heights, two samples per cell; a splatmap
  finer than that is averaged near the eye and coarser still at a distance.
- **Ground Blend fades into what is behind it.** Where nothing is close behind the surface (a rock's side seen against a
  distant valley) there is no seam to hide. A wall just behind a Ground Blend object counts as close. Two Ground Blend
  objects do not fade into each other, since neither is in the scene copy the other reads. It requires float render
  targets; without them it draws as a plain Model material.
- **`snowCover` is screen-space.** It has no depth: it does not round an edge or bury a stone, and covers only what the
  camera sees.
