`TimeOfDay` places the sun from an hour angle, a latitude and a solar declination (the season), producing the correct
shape of a day: a long, high arc in summer, a short, low one in winter, with sunrise toward the east. Its effect on
lights, sky and fog is expressed, like [Weather](/docs/graphics/weather/), as **multipliers of the values the scene
already authors**, so the same hour fits any project.

## Component

Add `TimeOfDay` to one entity.

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `hour` | Hour, 0–24 (12 is noon; default 12). Advanced by the system during play; set in the editor to preview an hour. |
| `daySeconds` | Real seconds per full day during play. 0 (default) holds the hour. |
| `latitude` | Latitude in degrees, positive north (default 40): how high the noon sun climbs and how long the day is. |
| `declination` | Season, −23.5 to 23.5; positive is northern summer (default 15). |
| `north` | Rotation of the world from compass north (+Z) toward +X, in degrees: the direction of sunrise (default 0). |
| `drive` | What the hour drives: Lights, Sky, Fog, Exposure, Reflections. All five by default. |

## In the editor

Details shows the day as a curve instead of a list of numbers: the sun's elevation over 24 hours, computed from this
component's latitude and declination (a winter day is a low, short arc; a polar summer day never sets), with a handle that
sets the hour. Alternatively, focus the dial and use the arrow keys: 15 minutes per step, one hour with **Shift**. Beside
the curve are the sun's elevation, the daylight value and the key-light scale at that hour; below it, a switch for each
drive flag. The hour set in the editor is previewed; it advances only in Play.

## What the hour drives

| Drive | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Lights** | The key light — the scene's directional light that follows a panorama's sun or casts shadows, otherwise the first — is aimed at the sun and scaled by a daylight curve: warm and weak near the horizon, off shortly below it. While the sun is down, it is aimed at the **moon**, given a few hundredths of the sun's strength and a blue tint, so night has shadows cast from the moon. The crossover happens where both are dark, so nothing pops. The ambient light is scaled and tinted: blue at night, warm at dusk. |
| **Reflections** | Baked reflection probes are dimmed and desaturated, since they were baked under a daytime sky. |
| **Sky** | A [Sky Atmosphere](/docs/graphics/post-processing/sky-and-weather/#sky-atmosphere) effect scatters sunlight through the atmosphere — Rayleigh scattering for the blue of the air, Mie for haze and the glare round the sun — so the noon blue, sunset red and afterglow come from one calculation. It draws the sun (white overhead, red at the horizon), the moon, the stars, and volumetric clouds lit by the sun and moved by the wind. It takes the sun from the clock, so it never mistakes the moonlit key light for the sun. |
| **Fog** | Height Fog's colour is lit as the day is (dark at night, orange at dusk), and its glow follows the key light. |
| **Exposure** | Auto Exposure's compensation is raised at night to keep the image readable. A scene without Auto Exposure gets the compensation on its Colour Grade. |

In the editor, scene lights are not modified — as with Weather, previewing an hour never changes the saved scene — so
the effects that follow the sun (sky, fog) receive the clock's sun position directly. Lights move during play.

## With weather

Time of day and weather scale the same lights, each from the authored values, so they compose: a rainy dusk is the
dusk's colour × the rain's strength. Weather also greys the sky (`overcast`), hiding the sun, moon and stars as clouds
thicken.

## Code

```ts
import { timeOfDayAt, currentTimeOfDay, TimeOfDay } from 'esengine';

// The sun at 18:30, latitude 51, midsummer.
const evening = timeOfDayAt(18.5, 51, 23);
evening.sunDir;        // unit vector toward the sun
evening.daylight;      // 0 at night, 1 by day

// Set the hour; the system applies the rest.
world.insert(clockEntity, TimeOfDay, { hour: 6, daySeconds: 600 });
```

| Function | Description |
|---|---|
| `timeOfDayAt(hour, latitude?, declination?, north?)` | The state at an hour (defaults 40, 15, 0), including `sunDir` and `daylight`. |
| `currentTimeOfDay()` | The current state of the scene's clock, or `null` without a `TimeOfDay`. |
| `hourAt(hour, daySeconds, t)` | The hour of a clock that started at `hour`, `t` seconds later. |

The hour derives from the scene clock, as weather does; `seekSceneClock(t)` moves the clock (see
[Scene clock](/docs/graphics/weather/#scene-clock)).

## Limits

- **Baked lighting is one moment.** Lightmaps, probe volumes and baked reflection probes are baked under a daytime sky,
  so lighting that does not come from lights does not move with the sun. A scene meant to pass through a whole day needs
  a live sun and sky — lit by lights, or baked mostly from soft sky light — and the dimming and tinting above handle the
  rest.
- **Emissive surfaces are not switched.** Windows and lamps do not light themselves at night; give them an emissive
  material or a light switched on from `currentTimeOfDay()?.daylight`.
