This page covers what scripts and physics read from the [water](/docs/graphics/water/overview/) surface, how objects disturb it,
the view from under it, and the Water shader.

![The Terrain & Water example: a river running to the sea, with floating crates](@/assets/guides/en/play-terrain-water.png)

## Asking the surface

The `WatersRes` resource (`esengine/water`) answers from the zone's baked field and the same wave formula the shader
draws, evaluated at the frame time.

```ts
import { WatersRes } from 'esengine/water';

const waters = app.getResource(WatersRes);
const s = waters.sample(x, y, z);
if (s && s.depth < 0) {
    // the point is -s.depth world units under the surface
}
```

| Method | Returns |
|---|---|
| `sample(x, y, z)` | A `WaterSample` (below), or `null` over no water (outside every shore, past the zone with no ocean) or inside an exclusion volume. |
| `heightAt(x, z)` | World height of the surface, or `null`. |
| `depthAt(x, z, y)` | Depth of water at rest over world height `y`: positive under water, `null` where there is none. |
| `bodyAt(x, z)` | `{ entity, bodyId, form, level }`: the body a place belongs to and its rest level, or `null`. |
| `shoreAt(x, z)` | Distance to the water's edge across the ground: positive on land, negative on water. Past the zone it is the open sea's, deep in; `null` where nothing is baked. |
| `disturb(x, y, z, radius, strength)` | Adds a disturbance to the ripple grid; see [Ripples](#ripples). |
| `rippleAt(x, y, z)` | Height the ripples add at the point; see [Ripples](#ripples). |

| `WaterSample` field | Description |
|---|---|
| `entity`, `bodyId` | The body holding most of the water there (where two meet, the one the point is further inside), and its `bodyId`. |
| `depth` | Height of the point above the surface (negative under it), in world units. |
| `normal` | Surface normal, world space. |
| `surface` | World position on the surface directly above or below the point. |
| `flow` | Water velocity in world units per second (a river's current; zero for still water). |
| `waterDepth` | Depth from the rest surface to the ground (river bed, lake floor). Open sea with no known ground, and sea past the zone, return 1e6. |
| `audioIntensity` | Loudness for water sound: a river's `audioIntensity` along its spline, 1 for still water. |
| `inside` | Always `true` for a non-null result. |

`sample` includes the waves and the surf at the heights the field gives, following the quality level (see
[Quality levels](/docs/graphics/water/overview/#quality-levels)). It does not include ripples, which follow the camera and differ
between machines. Waves shorter than the finest mesh cell can hold are in the shading only and are excluded. Near the
horizon, where the coarse mesh draws fewer short waves, the drawn surface and `sample` can differ by those short waves.

## Floating

`Floating` on an entity with a `RigidBody3D` and a collider makes the water hold it up and carry it. It applies forces
from `Waters.sample` at a few points of the body, so it needs no renderer: a server, a test or a headless build floats
objects identically.

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `buoyancy` | Water density relative to the body's (0–20, default 2). 1 holds the body up exactly when fully submerged; 2 floats it half under. |
| `drag` | Resistance to motion through the water, per second (default 1). Brings a body to rest in still water and carries it with a current. |
| `radius` | Depth below the surface, in world units, at which a point is fully wet. |
| `points` | Sample points in the body's own space, in world units. Spread them (hull corners) and waves tip the body. Empty: the origin. |
| `wake` | Ripple strength the body makes in water with ripples (0–10). 0 makes none. |

At each point the submerged share (0 at the surface, 1 from `radius` down) produces a buoyant force against gravity of
`mass * g * buoyancy * share / points`, applied at the point, and a drag against the point's velocity relative to the
water (the body's velocity plus the contribution of its rotation at the point, minus the current). Forces are applied
every fixed step before the physics world steps. A body with `buoyancy` 2 and one point settles with its centre
`radius / 2` below the surface.

It requires the 3D physics module built with `_physics3d_addForce` and `_physics3d_getBodyDynamics`; without them it
warns once and floats nothing. Floating models one rigid body in one fluid: there is no added mass, no hydrodynamic lift
from hull shape, and a fully submerged body is pushed only by the current.

## Ripples

When the zone's `ripples` is above 0, the zone keeps a grid of disturbances: a square window of the surface `rippleSize`
world units wide with `ripples` cells per side, stepped with the wave equation. A bump spreads as a ring at
`rippleSpeed`, loses `rippleDamping` of its height per second, and fades. The grid also holds **foam**: a disturbance
feeds it and it fades at `foamFade` per second, so a splash leaves lingering white water.

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `ripples` | Cells per side (0–256). 0 keeps none. Clamped to the quality level's `waterRippleCells`. |
| `rippleSize` | Window side in world units. The window follows the camera. |
| `rippleSpeed` | Spread speed, in world units per second. |
| `rippleDamping` | Share of height lost per second. |
| `foamFade` | Rate at which foam fades, per second. |

- **Sources.** `Waters.disturb(x, y, z, radius, strength)` adds a bump of `strength` world units (negative is a dip)
  over `radius`; it does nothing where there is no water or no ripple grid. A `Floating` body disturbs the water itself
  when it enters, leaves or moves along the surface, by its speed × `wake`.
- **Effect on the surface.** The Water shader reads the grid: ripple slope bends the normal used for lighting,
  reflection and refraction; foam whitens the surface; and ripple height lifts the vertices where the mesh is fine
  enough to carry them (from three mesh cells per shortest ripple, fully from six). Coarser rings keep them in the
  shading only.
- **Stepping.** The grid is stepped on the GPU in fixed substeps (each short enough that a wave crosses at most half a
  cell) over a pair of RGBA16F targets, and is carried along the current where the zone has one, so a wake in a river
  drifts downstream. A wave reaching the window edge leaves without reflecting.
- **Window.** The window follows the active camera in whole cells, so water of any size has ripples where they are seen
  for the cost of one grid shared by all bodies. What the window leaves is discarded; what it moves onto is still water.
- **Reading back.** `Waters.rippleAt(x, y, z)` returns the ripple height from a packed copy read back from the GPU, one or
  two frames old. Use it for visuals (a float bobbing on a wake), not physics; `Waters.sample` excludes ripples.

The grid holds heights up to `max(2 * tallest waves, rippleSize / 50)` (tallest waves in the field × the weather's
multiplier); a continuing disturbance saturates there. A cell is `rippleSize / ripples` world units, so ripples narrower
than one or two cells are not drawn. Cost scales with `ripples`² per substep: 128 cells is about 16 000.

## Under the surface

When the active camera is under a body of water, the water module reports the camera's position each frame (from
`Waters.sample`) and the post-process volume system adds an **Underwater** effect for as long as it is submerged, as a
weather adds its rain.

- **Fog by water crossed.** Each pixel's light has crossed the distance from the eye to its source (for the sky, `u_range`,
  the longest distance seen through water). The water absorbs it per channel, red first, by the same `u_absorption` and
  `u_density` the surface material uses, and adds its own colour. A white wall under water appears blue-green, more so
  further away.
- **Depth dimming.** The water's own colour dims with camera depth (`u_lightFalloff`), since light arrives from above.
- **Waterline across the lens.** Each pixel tests whether its point on the camera's near plane is below the surface, so a
  half-submerged lens shows a dry upper half and a wet lower half with a soft line (`u_band`, set to about 0.08 of the
  near-plane distance). Crossing the surface slides the line across the lens.
- **Caustics.** The renderer refracts sunlight through the current wave field and accumulates transmitted flux at
  several receiver depths. The bed and submerged objects sample this field at their positions; surface orientation,
  shadows and absorption along the optical path determine the light reaching each receiver.
- **Light shafts.** The water along each view ray scatters the sun toward the camera. The caustic pattern is marched along
  the ray (`waterShaftSteps`: 24 at High, 16 at Medium and Low), each step lit where the shadow map shows sun and by how
  much water lies above it (`u_shaftStrength`, `u_shaftRange`).
- **Authored effects take precedence.** A scene that authors an `underwater` effect in a volume keeps its values; only the
  camera depth and immersion are filled in.

Seen from below, the surface refracts the other way: rays from inside the water beyond the critical angle (about 49°) are
totally reflected, so the sky appears as a window overhead within a mirror, with the Fresnel term following. The window's
edge is where refraction runs out, not a drawn circle. The mirror shows the world below: the reflected ray is marched
through the copied scene (`waterReflectionSteps`, up to 20 growing steps) and the colour it meets is reflected, so an
object on the bottom appears in the surface above it. Where the ray meets nothing on screen, the mirror shows the light
the water itself scatters (sky and sun light returned in the water's colour).

Seen from above, the Water shader lights what is under the water through the surface: it finds where the refracted ray
ends, where the sun's ray to that point crossed the surface, and lights it by the caustic pattern there and the shadow
map, so the pattern bends with the waves.
### Per-body underwater settings

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `underwaterEnabled` | Whether a camera under this body sees the underwater look. Off for water the game never goes under. |
| `underwaterLook` | **Zone Material**: the look of the zone water material. **Own**: the settings below, so a murky pond and a clear sea in one zone each look their own. |
| `underwaterColor` | Colour the water gives back, deepening with camera depth. |
| `underwaterAbsorption` | Per-channel absorption as light crosses the water (red first in clear sea water). |
| `underwaterDensity` | Density per metre (0–4). 0.12 is clear sea water: with the default absorption, 10 m keeps about 58 % of red, 90 % of green and 94 % of blue. |
| `underwaterCaustics` | Caustic brightness on submerged surfaces. 0 draws none. |
| `underwaterPriority` | Where the camera is under two bodies at once, the higher priority's look is laid over the lower's by its blend weight. |
| `underwaterBlendRadius` | Distance past the outline over which the body's look fades in, blending looks when swimming between bodies. |
| `underwaterBlendWeight` | Amount of this body's look, 0–1. |

## Water shader

`builtin:water` also works standalone on any mesh. With `u_waveSpace` at its default (0) waves are measured on the world
xz plane, nothing is lifted, and the surface is assumed flat. The zone sets it to 1: the mesh's UV is its x and z,
`u_origin` is where the mesh was moved as it follows the camera, and vertices are lifted to the zone's water and waves.

**Waves.** `u_waveLength`, `u_waveHeight`, `u_waveSpeed` and `u_waveDirection` are hidden in the material editor. On a
zone surface the zone writes them every frame (its own length, speed and heading, and the weather's share as the height,
which each place's field height multiplies).

| Group | Parameters | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Ripples | `u_rippleStrength`, `u_rippleLength` | Wind ripples riding on the waves: three layers of a tiling ripple map (`default(ripples)`, or the material's `u_detailMap`) at mutually non-multiple sizes, each carried downwind, with mipmaps flattening what a pixel cannot resolve. |
| Ripples | `u_rippleTile`, `u_rippleFineSize`, `u_rippleWideSize` | Map repeat in ripple lengths; the other two layers' sizes as shares of it. |
| Ripples | `u_rippleWeight`, `u_rippleFineWeight`, `u_rippleWideWeight`, `u_rippleTurn` | Each layer's contribution, and how far off the wind two layers run (degrees). |
| Ripples | `u_rippleGusts`, `u_gustCalm`, `u_gustRuffle`, `u_gustSize` | Gusts ruffle the water in slow patches with slicks between: gust amount (0 for an even sea), ripple multipliers in slicks and ruffled patches, and patch size. |
| Ripples | `u_flowCycle` | Lifetime, in seconds, of each of the two surface copies carried down a current before the other takes over. |
| Colour | `u_bodyShading` | How much ripples also shade the water's own colour (0.25; at 1 every ripple darkens its far side, like a painted surface). The water colour is lit as light from under the surface, and the surface mirrors by the ripples. |
| Surface | `u_reflectance` | Reflectance at normal incidence (water: 0.0204). |
| Depth | `u_density`, `u_absorption` | Absorption of light along the water column, red first. The column is the depth under the pixel along the slope light takes in water (at most 49° from vertical), not the eye's line to the bed, so a lagoon seen at a low angle is as clear as from above. |
| Depth | `u_shallowColor`, `u_deepColor`, `u_deepening` | The water's own colour from shallow to deep, and how soon it turns (a share of density; lower keeps a lagoon turquoise over deeper water). |
| Depth | `u_bedShade` | How much the bed is dimmed by the water its light came down through (1, as water does; 0 lights it as dry). |
| Surface | `u_fallbackDepth` | Depth used everywhere when float render targets are unavailable. |
| Detail | `u_detailMap` | Two tiled normal maps in one texture, read as data: red/green are the first map's x/z slope (0.5 flat), blue/alpha the second's. |
| Detail | `u_detailTile0`, `u_detailTile1` | World units per repeat of each map. |
| Detail | `u_detailStrength0`, `u_detailStrength1` | Slope multiplier of each map (0–8). 0 leaves it out, which is the default. |
| Detail | `u_detailFlow0`, `u_detailFlow1` | Drift of each map, world units per second (vec2). |
| Foam | `u_foamWidth`, `u_foamCrest` | Foam where the water column is shallower than `u_foamWidth` (shores, rocks, pier legs), and on wave crests, broken up by moving noise. On a zone surface the baked shore also foams within `u_foamWidth` of the shore or of shallow water, where depth cannot be measured. |
| Caustics | `u_causticStrength`, `u_causticScale`, `u_causticSpeed` | Receiver light strength from either side. With a positive `waterCausticFieldSize`, wave shape, time and sunlight move the pattern; scale, speed and noise layers apply to the procedural approximation (field size 0). |

**Detail glints.** The detail maps produce the sun's glints on small slopes. Glints are the lit specular response of
those slopes, not a white texture, so they appear where sun, eye and slope align and fade with the light. They are
fragment-only: geometry and `Waters.sample` do not include them.

**Refraction and depth.** What lies under the water is the copied opaque scene, offset by the waves in proportion to the
water's depth. Depth is **measured**: before water is drawn the frame copies scene depth, and the water depth at a pixel
is the difference between that and the water's own distance.

**Reflection.** Sky, lights and environment reflect with a Fresnel term (IOR 1.333): clear looking down, mirror-like at
grazing angles. Add Screen Space Reflections to the volume for reflections of the scene itself.

**Rain.** When a `Weather` drives rain, drops ring on the surface (`u_rain`, written by the zone): rings that spread and
fade, a few per `u_rippleLength`, more as rain intensifies, with the surface slightly rougher. Rings are drawn, not
simulated: they add nothing to the ripple grid, make no foam, and fade with distance.

**Zone-written parameters.** The zone and the quality system write these; they are hidden and not authored:
`u_waveSpace`, `u_origin`, `u_waveGravity`, `u_metre`, `u_rain`, `u_rainRing`, `u_cellSize`; the baked field
(`u_zoneOn`, `u_zoneHeights`, `u_zoneSurfaceData`, `u_zoneExposure`, `u_zoneOrigin`, `u_zoneSize`, `u_zoneHeight`,
`u_zoneRange`); the ocean past the region (`u_oceanOn`, `u_oceanLevel`, `u_oceanWaves`, `u_oceanLook`); surf
(`u_surfHeight`, `u_surfLength`, `u_surfSpeed`); the tiled mesh (`u_tileMorph`, `u_tileEye`, `u_waterMeshView`,
`u_farSurface`, `u_geoWaves`, 0 for lite water); quality (`u_waveCount`, `u_mirror`, `u_causticOn`, `u_reflectSteps`,
`u_reflectGrowth`, `u_causticLayers`); and ripples (`u_rippleOn`, `u_rippleMap`, `u_rippleOrigin`, `u_rippleSize`,
`u_rippleTexel`).

## Limits

- **Caustics and shafts use the first directional light.** The local projected field spans three wavelengths around the
  primary view, with sixteen depth slices spaced one eighth of a wavelength apart, and fades at its edge. Opaque depth
  supplies the receiver position and normal.
- **The mirror from below is screen-space.** It shows only what is on screen; a ray that leaves the picture or meets
  nothing shows the light the water itself scatters.
- **Ripples are one camera window.** Water far from the camera keeps none, and in a split screen both views share the
  first camera's window.
- **Floating bodies only.** Objects float and leave wakes; there is no swimming behaviour.
