This page covers the shape-specific behaviour of [water bodies](/docs/graphics/water/overview/#water-bodies): where lakes and the
ocean meet the ground, how bodies cut the terrain, rivers along splines, and waterfalls.

![The river "River In" selected in the Terrain & Water example: its Spline (6 points) and Water Body (Form River) cards in Details](@/assets/guides/en/water-river-handles.png)
## Lakes and shores

A lake is its outline at its entity's height. The zone computes the signed distance to the outline and, with a `Terrain`
in the scene, the depth of water over it; from these it derives the share of the waves each point keeps (none at the
edge or in shallow water, all from `shoreWidth` in). The surface is cut along the outline (traced at the zone's
resolution, not the mesh's cells), geometry and shading calm toward the shore, and the shore foams.

The water fills low ground and meets hills where they cross it; waves die out as it shallows. A body is water only where
its surface is above its ground: a hill or bank inside a lake outline or river width that stands above the water is land,
both for rendering and for queries. Over no terrain, a lake has unlimited depth. The outline must not self-intersect.

The ocean takes its shore from the terrain the same way: inside the zone it is cut where the ground stands above its
level, and its waves calm over `shoreWidth` of depth. The ocean is the open sea beyond the zone's edge: low ground is sea
only where low ground connects it to that edge, so a hollow below sea level enclosed by higher ground stays dry (or holds
a lake of its own). Beyond the zone the ocean is everywhere at its level with full waves.

Where bodies overlap, a lake holds the water over a river (a river entering a lake takes the lake's level there, its
current fading into still water) and both hold it over the ocean. The water surface casts no shadow: the sun lights what
is under it through the caustics.

## Terrain shaping

Lakes, rivers and islands change the terrain under and around them when `affectsTerrain` is on (default). Changes are
laid as [terrain modifiers](/docs/graphics/terrain/rendering-and-code/#modifiers), so the drawn ground, its queries and
collision all follow, and editing a body rebuilds only the ground it covers.

- A **lake** with `bedDepth` cuts its floor under its outline, sloping down from the shore.
- A **river** with `bedDepth` (or per-point `depth`, see [Splines](#splines)) cuts a trough `bedDepth` below the surface at its middle, reaching the surface at its edge;
  the banks continue at the same slope until they meet the ground, at most two widths out. Each stretch of river is one
  modifier, so editing the spline rebuilds only the ground along it. Ground already lower than the bed is left alone.
- An **island** raises the ground inside its outline to its own height, with its slope running down over `shoreWidth`.
- Ground a game carves itself (a heightmap that already contains its rivers) keeps `bedDepth` at 0, or `affectsTerrain`
  off.

`channelShape` selects the cut profile: **Built In** (above), or **Curve Channel**, configured by the fields below.

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `elevationCurve` | Bed profile across the ramp: from the curve start (0, at the shore plus `channelEdgeOffset`) to the ramp end (1), the share of full depth cut. Keep its end at 1 so the body's depth (or a river point's) is reached. |
| `channelEdgeOffset` | Distance in from the shore where the curve starts. Negative starts it on the bank, widening the cut past the water. |
| `curveRampWidth` | Width over which the curve ramps from its start to full depth. |
| `terrainBlend` | **Alpha Blend** (default): cuts higher ground and raises lower ground, so the water always has ground at its level. **Min**: cuts only (for a river entering a lake, so it does not raise the lake floor). **Max**: raises only. **Additive**: adds the cut a flat ground would receive, keeping the ground's own detail and slope but not exact bed heights. |
| `falloffMode` | **Width**: the bank returns to the terrain over `falloffWidth`. **Angle**: the bank runs out at `falloffAngle` until it meets the ground, at most `falloffWidth`. |
| `falloffWidth`, `falloffAngle` | Falloff width (or maximum reach), and bank slope in degrees from level. |
| `falloffEdgeOffset` | Flat edge at water level before the falloff starts: a level bank even where a river cuts through a hill. |
| `terrainZOffset` | Raises (or, negative, lowers) the whole cut relative to the water surface. |
| `terraceAlpha`, `terraceSpacing`, `terraceSmoothness` | Terraces over the cut: amount (0 none, 1 fully stepped), rise per terrace, and edge roundness (0 sheer, 1 no steps). |
| `terraceMaskLength`, `terraceMaskStartOffset` | Restrict terraces to a span of distance out from the water's edge, starting at the offset. Length 0 terraces everywhere. |
| `blurRadius` | Averages the cut over this radius, softening edges and corners. 0: none. |
| `curlAmount1`, `curlTiling1`, `curlAmount2`, `curlTiling2` | Two octaves of curl noise bending the cut outline: displacement and swirl size. |
| `innerSmoothDistance`, `outerSmoothDistance` | Rounds the inner edge (bed to bank) over a width, and the outer edge (bank to ground) over a height. |

All lengths are in world units.

## Basins and drainage

Placing a world's lakes and rivers is the game's (or its generator's) decision. `findBasins(grid, ground, { seaLevel })`
(`esengine/water`) provides the terrain analysis from one priority flood over a grid:

| Result | Description |
|---|---|
| `basins` | Each hollow, with the `level` water would fill it to (the lowest point of its rim), its `deepest` point, and its `outlet` where it overflows. |
| `filled` | Height water would stand at over each texel. |
| `basin` | Which basin each texel belongs to. |
| `downstream`, `accumulation` | Where each texel drains to (down to the region edge) and how much drains through it — where rivers run. |
| `sea` | With a sea level, the low ground the sea reaches from the edge. |

`drainPath(map, x, z)` follows the drainage from a point. A lake placed at a basin's `level` over its outline fills it to
the brim; a river from its `outlet` along `drainPath` runs where water would.

## Rivers

A river follows the `Spline` on its own entity: its width is the curve's width at each point and its surface is the
curve's height at the nearest point, so a river runs downhill. Put a `Spline` and a `WaterBody` with `form` **River** on
one entity and the zone bakes it.

- **Current.** `flowSpeed` is the speed where the river is at its widest. A narrower reach runs faster and a wider one
  slower, between a quarter and double that speed, as the same discharge through a different width. The direction is
  the curve's. The current fades in over one width from the river's start and out over one width into whatever it
  enters, so a river mouth is still water. A spline point's `velocity` overrides `flowSpeed` locally.
- **Advected surface.** The shader carries waves and ripples downstream: two copies of the surface move along the
  current a cycle at a time, each fading in as the other fades out, so the reset of one is hidden and the surface does
  not stretch at bends.
- **Waves.** A river has its own `waveHeight`, calmed toward its banks over `shoreWidth`, like any body. `Waters.sample`
  returns the current in `flow` (world units per second, along the river; zero elsewhere).
- **Banks and mouths.** A bank is where the field's shore distance crosses zero, traced at the zone's resolution. Where a
  river enters a lake, the sea or another river, the two are blended over one and a half texels: no seam, no shore across
  the mouth, no step in height.

## Splines

`Spline` is data; nothing draws it. A river reads the spline on its entity; any other system that follows a path can
read it with `SplineCurve` (`esengine/spline`, an optional module).

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `points` | Points the curve passes through, in order, in the entity's own space. |
| `closed` | Joins the end to the start (three or more points). |

| Point property | Description |
|---|---|
| `x`, `y`, `z` | Position in the entity's space. |
| `width` | Width of whatever follows the curve here, eased to the next point's. |
| `type` | Segment to the next point: `curve` (default), `customTangent`, `linear` or `constant`. |
| `arriveTangent`, `leaveTangent` | With `customTangent`: direction and pull entering and leaving the point. |
| `depth`, `velocity`, `audioIntensity` | Optional per-point river values, eased along the curve. Absent: the body's `bedDepth` and `flowSpeed`, and loudness 1. |

```ts
import { SplineCurve } from 'esengine/spline';

const curve = new SplineCurve(spline.points, spline.closed);
curve.position(s);   // the point at distance s along the curve
curve.tangent(s);    // direction of travel there
curve.width(s);      // width there, eased between points
curve.nearest(x, z); // nearest place in the xz plane: s, distance, side
```

The default segment is a centripetal Catmull-Rom curve: it passes through every point and does not loop or cusp where
points are unevenly spaced. It is parameterised by distance, so `s` is in world units from the start rather than a
parameter that runs fast where points are close. Beyond either end an open curve clamps to the end; a closed curve
wraps.

In Details, the Spline section lists the point count, steps through points (first, previous, next, last), and edits the
selected point's input key, location, type, tangents, width and, on a river, depth, velocity and audio intensity.

## Waterfalls

A river's surface is a field over the ground and cannot stand vertically, so a river reaching a cliff stops at its edge.
A **`Waterfall`** continues from there. Place it on an entity at the lip, facing the direction the water leaves (its
forward). Water is thrown level at `speed` and pulled down by gravity; each sheet ends where it meets the ground, water
other than the water it left, or `maxDrop` below the lip. It is drawn as the entity's own `MeshRenderer` and rebuilt when
its fields, its position or what lies under it change.
| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `width` | Width of the water at the lip. |
| `speed` | Exit speed, level along the entity's forward. Faster throws the water further from the cliff. |
| `flare` | Extra width at a sheet's foot, as a share of its width (1 is twice as wide). |
| `sheets`, `sheetGap` | Number of sheets (1–5), one behind another along the throw, and their spacing at the lip. The middle sheet is full width; the others are narrower. |
| `maxDrop` | Drop below the lip at which a fall that meets nothing ends. |
| `rows`, `across` | Rows down a sheet (2–256) and cells across it (1–32). |

Only the entity's rotation about the vertical aims the fall; keep it upright and unscaled.

**Waterfall shader.** With no material on the `MeshRenderer`, the stock **Waterfall** shader is used: clear water at the
lip that tears into strands and then veils, the pattern travelling down with the water.

| Parameter | Description |
|---|---|
| `u_water`, `u_foam` | Colour at the lip, and the white it breaks into. |
| `u_strands` | Strands across half a sheet. |
| `u_run` | Times per second of fall a strand changes (fewer: longer strands). |
| `u_veil`, `u_tearBy` | Share of the sheet gone at its foot, and how far down it is torn that much. |
| `u_opacity`, `u_roughness` | Opacity and roughness. |
| `u_translucency` | Light transmitted through the sheet, lit from whichever side the sun is on. 0 lights it as an opaque surface. |

A custom material can read the mesh data: `uv.x` across the sheet, `uv.y` seconds the water there has been falling,
vertex colour red the share of the fall completed, green a per-sheet number.

### WaterfallAnchor

Content belonging to a place on the fall is a child entity with **`WaterfallAnchor`**. The fall keeps that child on the
middle of its path, `lift` above it:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `at` | Position on the fall as a share of the fall time: 0 the lip, 1 (default) the foot, wherever the water lands. |
| `lift` | Height above the fall's path. |

A [`ParticleEmitter`](/docs/world/particles/) on the child provides mist, which moves with the foot when the ground or the
water under the fall changes:

```json
{ "name": "Foot Mist", "parent": 31, "components": [
  { "type": "Transform", "data": {} },
  { "type": "WaterfallAnchor", "data": { "at": 1 } },
  { "type": "ParticleEmitter", "data": { "shape": 5, "shapeRadius": 700, "rate": 22, "speedMin": 150, "speedMax": 600,
      "lifetimeMin": 4, "lifetimeMax": 8, "startSizeMin": 400, "startSizeMax": 800, "endSizeMin": 1400, "endSizeMax": 2400,
      "blendMode": 0, "startColor": { "r": 1, "g": 1, "b": 1, "a": 0.12 }, "endColor": { "r": 1, "g": 1, "b": 1, "a": 0 } } } ] }
```

The anchor sets the child's position only, in the fall's own space; its rotation and scale are left as authored.

## Limits

- **Lake outlines are planar polygons.** They do not follow a curved shore.
- **No flow-driven foam.** Rapids and falls are not foamed by water speed or steepness.
- **Beds are cut only on request.** A lake cuts its floor only with `bedDepth` above zero; a river only where `bedDepth`
  or a point's `depth` is above zero. Both require `affectsTerrain`.
