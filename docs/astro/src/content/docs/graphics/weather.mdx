A scene's weather is not a pile of effect parameters. A project's fog is in its own units and its sun is as bright
as its author made it, so a weather is expressed as **multipliers of what the scene already says** — the sun at a
quarter of its strength, the fog five times as thick, the picture a little greyer — plus a few levels that mean the same
everywhere: how hard it rains, how wet the ground is. The same preset then fits any project.

## Start from the whole environment

The parts of an outdoor scene point at one another: the clock moves the sun, the sky draws clouds that cast shadows, the fog reads those
clouds so a gap in them is a beam, and the weather scales it all. **Create → Rendering → Outdoor Environment** makes the working arrangement
at once, in one root: a sun, a sky light, a clock, a weather and a global volume with the sky, the haze and a plain grade, set for the engine's
own scale of a hundred units to the metre. Change a number in any of them, or delete what you do not want; it is an ordinary set of entities.

## The component

Add a `Weather` component to one entity.

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| `preset` | The weather the scene is in: Clear, Mist, Overcast, Rain, Storm. Default Clear. |
| `target` | The weather it is turning into; None for none (the default). |
| `progress` | How far along that turn it is (0–1, default 0). The game advances it; set it in the editor to preview a half-turn. |
| `duration` | Seconds a turn takes (default 6). |
| `sequence` | Presets to cycle through by themselves, comma separated — `clear,mist,rain,clear`. Empty (the default) stays put. |
| `hold` | Seconds each weather of the sequence holds before the next turn (default 20). |
| `drive` | What the weather drives: Lights, Fog, Rain, Grade, Exposure, Reflections, Sound, Wind. All by default. |
| `soundSource` | An entity with a looping `AudioSource` — the sound of the rain. The weather raises its `playing` while it rains and sets its volume to the authored one times how hard it rains. |

A turn is eased, so it starts and ends gently, and a finished turn lands on its target and clears itself.

## In the editor

The Details panel shows the five built-in weathers as buttons (a project's own preset id still works and shows as Custom), the
move toward the next one as a progress you can drag, the cycle and what the weather drives, and a readout of what it is doing
to the picture right now — the key light's scale, the fog's, the wetness and the rain. Rain and fog reach the picture through a
Post Process Volume, which the panel says.

## What a weather changes

- **Lights.** Directional lights are scaled (and tinted) and the ambient light is scaled, from the strengths they were
  authored with — the weather scales your number, never its own last answer — and they are put back when the weather
  is taken away or `Lights` is switched off. Only while the game runs: in the editor the lights are left alone, so
  previewing a weather never changes the scene you save.
- **Shadows.** The directional light's shadows get softer (a source `shadowSoftness` suns wide) and lighter (`shadowStrength` times
  the authored darkness) as the sky clouds over, the way an overcast sky's shadows fade into the ground.
- **Fog.** Height Fog's density, its sun glow and the volumetric light it carries are scaled, its sky haze is raised, and
  its colour moves toward the weather's.
- **Rain.** The Rain effect is brought with the weather (when the scene has none) at the preset's strength, and Rain and
  Screen Space Reflections are given the wetness and puddles it leaves. Where the scene authored its own rain, the
  weather can only raise it.
- **Reflections.** A baked reflection probe shows the sky it was baked under, so a rainy street would mirror a sunny one.
  The ambient light carries `reflectionScale` and `reflectionSaturation` for exactly this: what baked probes show is
  multiplied by the first and drained of colour by the second, and the weather turns both down.
- **Sky.** A Sky Atmosphere effect takes the preset's `overcast` as cloud coverage and haze: the clouds thicken and join up, and the sun, moon and stars behind them are hidden by the same transmittance.
- **Wind.** The preset's `wind` scales how far plants sway (a Foliage material's `u_windStrength`, a Sway sprite's `u_swayWind`) and `waves` scales the height of the waves on every body of water, in what is drawn and in what the surface answers.
- **Grade and exposure.** Colour Grade's saturation and contrast are scaled, and Auto Exposure's compensation raised.

The effects are applied after the scene's volumes have blended, so a scene that fogs itself thinly stays thinner than
one that fogs itself thickly in the same rain.

## Your own weathers

```ts
import { registerWeatherPreset, WEATHER_CLEAR } from 'esengine';

// A preset names only what it changes; everything else is neutral.
const sandstorm = registerWeatherPreset('sandstorm', {
  sun: 0.5, fogDensity: 10, fogTint: [0.82, 0.62, 0.38], fogTintAmount: 0.85,
  saturation: 0.8, exposure: 0.4,
});
```

A custom preset answers to its name in a `sequence`, and to the id `registerWeatherPreset` returns in `preset` and
`target`. Registering a built-in name again retunes it.

## Turning by code

The component is data: write `target` and `progress: 0` and the system does the rest.

```ts
world.insert(weatherEntity, Weather, { target: WEATHER_RAIN, progress: 0, duration: 10 });
```
