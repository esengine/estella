import { Aside } from '@astrojs/starlight/components';

A project can ship one picture and run it on a flagship phone and on a six-year-old one. **Quality levels** are how:
three profiles — **Low, Medium, High** — each saying what the renderer spends at that level. The project picks one
(or lets the device pick), and a game that is too slow for its level steps its resolution down on its own.

## Choosing the level

**Settings → Project → Rendering → Device Quality → Quality level:**

| Mode | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Default | Nothing chosen: **Medium on phones and tablets**, Off everywhere else. A mini game keeps its own defaults. |
| Off | No quality management, on every device. The project renders as authored. |
| Low / Medium / High | That level, on every device. |
| Auto | The level comes from the device: the first [device rule](#device-rules) that matches, otherwise memory and core count. |

**Dynamic resolution** lowers the scene's resolution under GPU pressure, down to the level's *Min render scale*, and
brings it back when there is room; the UI stays at display resolution. A scene drawn below the screen's size is stretched back with a Catmull-Rom resampler clamped to the texels around it, which keeps an edge steeper than a bilinear copy does without drawing a halo; a whole-number stretch is an exact copy instead. **Target frame rate** is the frame time it works
to.

## What a level holds

What a level spends is grouped by where it goes, twelve groups in all, and each group is a table: a row for each setting and
a column for each level, **Low, Medium and High side by side**, every cell editable.

![Project Settings, Rendering page: the quality levels, a table per group with Low, Medium and High side by side](@/assets/guides/en/settings-quality-levels.png)

| Group | Settings |
|---|---|
| Resolution & Anti-Aliasing | Render scale, Min render scale, Anti-aliasing (MSAA or Temporal), MSAA |
| View Distance & Draws | Depth pre-pass, Draw call budget, LOD distance scale, Occlusion grid, Batch scan interval |
| Lights & Shading | 3D lighting (Full or Direct) |
| Shadows | Atlas size, Cell size, Cascades, Distance, 2D soft-shadow samples (read by 2D lights only) |
| Global Illumination & Occlusion | Ambient occlusion resolution, Screen-space GI resolution, Voxel GI gather resolution, Cheaper dynamic GI; the ambient occlusion, GTAO, screen-space GI, voxel GI, dynamic GI and contact shadow effects |
| Reflections | Planar reflection scale, max size and updates per second; the screen-space reflections effect |
| Sky & Volumetrics | Still sky from a panorama, Fog volume resolution; the sky, fog, light shaft and weather effects |
| Post-Processing | A master switch, Lens flare resolution; every other effect |
| Effects & Particles | Particle limit, 2D rain shelter mask |
| Terrain & Foliage | Detail distance, Terrain surface page size and pages a frame, Terrain page atlas, Planet draw levels, Voxel rebuild budget, Plants that bend and that start bending a frame |
| Water | Water waves (1 to 8), Water ripple cells, the Displacement, Mirror and Caustics switches, Underwater light shaft steps, Water reflection steps, Caustic layers; the underwater effect. Shown once the water module is in the build |
| Textures & Memory | Texture streaming, its budget, smallest and starting sizes, shrink and unseen delays, interval and textures at once; Idle render targets; Terrain tile memory |

A group's effects are a matrix of effect by level, ticked where the level runs the effect. A changed cell is tinted and carries a
reset arrow, whose tip names the level's own default and which puts it back. A cell where the setting does nothing at that level
is dimmed: MSAA at a level that smooths with Temporal, an effect's resolution at a level with post-processing off.

![A level table with one changed cell: Medium's render scale at 0.75, tinted, with its reset arrow](@/assets/guides/en/settings-quality-changed.png)

<Aside type="note">
A cell is compared only with its own level's default, so the same number can be a change at Low and the default at Medium:
0.75 is Low's render scale, and a change at Medium and High.
</Aside>

In a settings window too narrow for three columns the page goes back to one level at a time: **Quality Levels → Editing level**
picks which level the rows show and change, and a row that does nothing at that level is hidden.

Each level starts from the engine's own values for it, and **only what you change is saved** in `project.esproject`
under `features.rendering.quality.profiles.<level>`. A default the engine improves later reaches a project that never
touched it, and a row set back to its level's default is not a difference.

**Anti-aliasing** is MSAA or Temporal. Temporal draws at one sample and lets the TAA pass smooth from the frames before:
far cheaper at 1080p and somewhat softer, it needs a post-process volume on the camera, and the level's MSAA cell is dimmed
while it is chosen. **Depth pre-pass** draws the opaque scene twice, depth alone first, so each pixel is shaded once for the surface in
front. It pays where surfaces overlap (a wood, a crowd of foliage) on a GPU that shades what it rasterises; on one that defers
shading to the end of a tile it is geometry paid for twice. It is off at Low and on at Medium and High. The default anti-aliasing is MSAA at Low (one sample) and High (4), and Temporal at Medium. The pre-pass and the colour pass declare the vertex position invariant on both WebGL2 and WebGPU, so a surface's colour always passes the equal-depth test against its own depth, including on Metal; a Mac that showed dark triangles on terrain with the pre-pass on needs nothing but an up-to-date build.

**3D lighting** is Full or Direct. Direct compiles every lit mesh as `#pragma lighting direct` does: the same lighting model with the sixteen lights of
the light block and hard shadows, without the lights past them, soft shadows or hair shadows. A full-screen lit floor on a phone costs about half as much
(see [Lighting on a phone](/docs/graphics/lighting/#lighting-on-a-phone)). Low and Medium are Direct, High is Full; sprites keep their 2D lighting either way.

**Detail distance** is the share of its detail distances the ground and the plants keep at the level: at 0.5, a tree that changes to its
cheaper mesh at 60 m and is not drawn as itself past 150 m does so at 30 m and 75 m, and its far cards take over sooner; a
Foliage field is drawn and keeps its finer blades half as far; and a terrain or a planet keeps its finest cells half as far
before the ground coarsens. Low keeps 0.5, Medium 0.8 and High all of it, so the same world draws a fraction of the triangles
on a phone.

**Draw call budget** is the most draws a frame at the level should ask of the GPU: 150 at Low, 400 at Medium and 1,500 at
High. Every pass counts, not only the scene's own list: each shadow cascade draws its casters again, the depth pre-pass draws
the opaque scene again, and post-processing adds a draw per pass. Nothing is held back to meet it. The Profiler's **Render**
module shows the frame's GPU draws against the budget and splits them by pass, and the stats overlay turns the count red past
it. A draw costs the CPU and the driver more than the GPU, and on a phone or in a mini game it is what a 3D scene runs out of
first. To bring it down, see [Static batching](/docs/performance/static-batching/) for many different meshes and fewer shadow
cascades for the shadow pass.

**LOD distance scale** multiplies the distances at which every LOD Group hands over to its next level and stops being drawn,
and the one where an impostor card stands in for its model: 0.6 at Low, 0.8 at Medium, 1 at High. It is applied to the size
on screen each group is measured by, so a group authored to change at 40 m changes at 24 m at Low; hysteresis and the levels a
group declares are untouched. A Foliage field's far versions are LOD Groups too, so at Low they take both this and the detail
distance.

**Occlusion grid** is the side of the grid the declared Occluders are drawn into on the CPU before each object's bounds are
tested against it: 64 at every level. A coarser grid is a quarter of the texels to fill, and an occluder must cover a texel
whole to hide anything, so it hides less: at 32 only an occluder wider than two of its texels (6% of the screen) hides what is
behind it. Filling the grid for `third-person-3d`'s two occluders took 0.14 ms a frame at 64 and 0.06 ms at 32 on a CPU slowed
five times, which no level needs to save at the price of drawing what an occluder would have hidden; a project can still set it. **Batch scan interval** is the frames between looks at a merged model and at a static batch for parts
that moved or went away: 60 at Low, 30 at Medium and High; `refresh()` still looks at once.

**Terrain surface page size** and **pages a frame** are the baked surface a terrain draws with Virtual Texture on: 128 texels a
page at Low and 256 at Medium and High (the minigame's half size, which was fixed, is now Low's), and one page baked a frame at
Low, two at Medium and High. **Terrain page atlas** is the side of the atlases a terrain and a planet keep their pages of heights
and paint in: 1024 at Low and 2048 at Medium and High. A quarter of the pages fit a 1024 atlas, so a ground that asks for more
is drawn at the share of its detail distance that fits, as it already was past 2048. **Planet draw levels** is the most levels
of a planet's tree drawn: a planet whose cell size asks for more is drawn no finer than that many levels down from a whole face,
while its collision and what code reads of its ground keep the cell size the Planet was given. **Voxel rebuild budget** is
the milliseconds of a frame a voxel terrain spends making again what an edit changed (2, 3 and 4), and **Plants that bend** and
**Plants starting to bend a frame** cap PlantInteraction (128 and 16, 256 and 32, 512 and 48); a game's own lower
`maxActive` or `maxActivatedPerFrame` is kept.

What these settings took off two examples, exported for the web and played headless at a phone's size (844×390 at 2.5×), the
counts the renderer reports, before these settings and after:

| Example, level | Triangles before → after | Texture memory before → after |
|---|---|---|
| `third-person-3d`, Low | 7,244 → 5,154 (LOD levels 3/12/6 → 0/7/14) | 13.1 MB → 13.1 MB |
| `third-person-3d`, Medium | 7,264 → 6,216 | 83.8 MB → 83.8 MB |
| `terrain-water`, Low | 598,283 → 555,275 | 373.1 MB → 337.1 MB (1024 page atlases) |
| `terrain-water`, Medium | 658,771 → 615,763 | 496.4 MB → 496.4 MB |

`bistro` and `dusk-fort`, which have no LOD Group, terrain or water, draw the same at Low and Medium as before, to the draw and the byte.

**Underwater light shaft steps**, **Water reflection steps** and **Caustic layers** are loops in the water's shaders, each
compiled at its High count and stopped early by the level: shafts walk 16, 16 and 24 samples a pixel; the surface's reflection
(from above, and its mirror from below) follows its ray 8, 12 and 20 steps, each longer than the one before by as much as it
takes for the fewer steps to reach as far as High's twenty, so what it finds is the same and lands less exactly; the web of light on what lies under the water lays 2, 3 and 3 of its scales. A shaft's samples are jittered per pixel,
so fewer show as grain rather than bands.

**Texture streaming** holds each texture that only meshes draw at the size its views need. A texture a MeshRenderer draws, as
its texture, its normal map or through its material, is first loaded no larger than 256 texels on a side, so a scene comes up in a
fraction of the memory; then, for every mesh a view draws, the renderer works out how many texels across its textures need for a
texel to be about a pixel there, from the mesh's own UV density, its size and its distance, and four times a second the textures
that are the wrong size are decoded again from their files at the next power of two above that and put behind the same handle. A
texture grows as soon as it is needed bigger and shrinks only after it has been needed smaller for three seconds; one no view
draws for five seconds goes down to 64 texels. It is on at Low and Medium and off at High; **Texture streaming budget** caps what
the streamed textures take together (128 MB at Low, 256 MB at Medium, 0 is no cap), and past it they are held a level softer.

Measured in the editor's Play at Low, in the `texture-streaming` editor check (the engine's own estimate of GPU memory):

| Example | Streaming off | On: first frame | On: ten seconds in | The two pictures, settled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dusk-fort` | 285.6 MB | 33.6 MB | 31.0 MB | mean difference 0.28 of 255 |
| `bistro` | 1293.5 MB | 321.1 MB | 449.6 MB (228 textures, 149 moves) | mean difference 1.1 of 255; 0.04% of pixels differ visibly |

**The download.** A web build ships a small copy beside each texture a mesh draws as its surface and that is more than 512 texels
on a side: no larger than 256, in the same format (a KTX2 encoded again in its own mode, a PNG or a JPEG written as what it was),
halved from the shipped file with the box filter the cook's own mip chains use. Streaming starts the texture from the copy, and
fetches the file itself only once a view needs more than the copy holds; a texture nothing comes close to is never downloaded
whole. Exported for the web at Low and run in a headless browser (software rendering, the files served as immutable):

| Example | Texture download, without the copies | With the copies | Files fetched whole |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dusk-fort`, 20 s | 44.8 MB | 14.9 MB (the copies are 0.4 MB of it) | 3 of 12 |
| `bistro`, 20 s | 35.3 MB | 16.1 MB (3.4 MB of copies) | 4 of 155 |
| `bistro`, 120 s | — | 18.8 MB | 15 of 155 |

A headless browser draws in software, a frame or two a second, so it grows textures far slower than a GPU does: `bistro` was still
fetching a file every five seconds or so at two minutes, and these are what was downloaded by then, not what it settles at. On a GPU
in the editor, ten seconds in, 40 of `bistro`'s textures had grown past their copies and their files come to 8.9 MB: with the 3.4 MB
of copies, about 12.4 MB of textures to download where a build without them takes 35.3 MB. The
copies cost the package their own size (430 KB beside `dusk-fort`'s 42.6 MB of textures). A mini game downloads its package whole,
so no copies are made for one; nor for a texture something other than a mesh draws.

What it does not do: until a view has asked for more, a texture is drawn at the size it started at: the first second or two of a scene can
be softer than the rest, and a move costs a decode and an upload on the frame it lands, which shows as a frame or two of tens of
milliseconds while a scene settles. A texture a sprite, a UI element, a tilemap, a particle or a trail also draws is put back at its
size and left there. A KTX2 file is cut at a level without decoding; where the host transcodes KTX2 itself (the native builds) it
cannot be, and the texture is left at its size.

Under the sea in `terrain-water` (the U key), at 2110×975 on a desktop GPU, the underwater pass took 6.28 ms with 24 shaft steps
and 4.31 ms with Medium's 16; with caustics turned on at Low it took 3.52 ms before, 2.08 ms with 16 and 0.35 ms with a single
step. Below 16 the per-pixel jitter shows as a fine regular grain on what lies under the water, at full resolution with nothing
after it to smooth it, so Low keeps 16.

The streaming's own pace is the level's too, each read only while streaming is on: **Streaming smallest size** (32 at Low, 64
at Medium and High), **Streaming shrink delay** (2 s at Low, 3 s), **Streaming unseen hold** (3 s at Low, 5 s), **Streaming
interval** (500 ms at Low, 250 ms), **Streaming textures at once** (2 at Low, 4) and **Streaming starting size** (128 at Low,
256). **Idle render targets** is the megabytes of render targets no pass is using that the renderer keeps to lend again: 64 at
Low, 128 at Medium and 256 at High; past it the longest idle are destroyed. **Terrain tile memory** is what a tiled terrain
keeps of its full-detail tiles: 64, 128 and 256 MB, the least recently used let go first and read again when wanted.

<Aside type="note">
How many loads run at once and how much a generated world keeps are not a level's: they follow the network and the package,
not the device's GPU. They are in **Project Settings → Game → Runtime → Loading & Streaming**: scene preload at once (6),
chunks made at once per streamed layer (16), ground pages asked at once (16), planet surface tiles kept (6,000), the world plan
cache (40,000) and the plans each planning worker keeps (24). Like the rest of the runtime block, only what differs from these is
written to `project.esproject`.
</Aside>

## Leaving effects out

Every post-process effect the engine has is ticked, per level, in the group it belongs to. An unticked effect is skipped at
that level wherever a camera's volume asks for it — and all of it: an effect with several passes, such as Ambient Occlusion or
Auto Exposure, is removed whole. The manifest holds the effects left out by type:

```json
"quality": { "mode": "auto", "profiles": { "low": { "skipEffects": ["ssr", "taa", "depthOfField"] } } }
```

By default Low leaves out reflections, temporal anti-aliasing, depth of field, light shafts, motion blur, lens flare, ambient
occlusion (SSAO and GTAO), subsurface scattering, voxel GI and dynamic GI; Medium leaves out reflections and depth of field;
High leaves out nothing. These defaults follow the work each effect does per pixel; they are **not** measured on weak devices.
The editor's `fx-cost` check measures each effect's cost on the machine it runs on, which is a way to find out what yours
costs before choosing what a level skips.

This list is the only thing that says whether ambient occlusion runs. A profile written before it may hold `"ssao": false`,
which is read as `ssao` and `gtao` in the list, or `"ssao": true`, which takes `ssao` off it; the editor writes the list
instead the next time that level's effects are ticked. A player's `quality.setPreferences({ ssao: false })` reads the same way.

## Effect resolution

Some effects already work at half the picture's size and are laid back over it, because what they work out is smooth. Each has
a resolution of its own, in the group the effect belongs to, which multiplies that size: 1 draws it as authored, at half the
picture on a side; 0.5 at a quarter and 0.25 at an eighth, down to the effect's own smallest size, below which its picture
breaks up. Nothing else moves: tone mapping, FXAA, grading and every other pass over the whole picture always run at the full
size, or the whole frame would go soft.

| Setting | Effect | Group | Smallest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ambient occlusion resolution (`aoResolution`) | SSAO and GTAO | Global Illumination & Occlusion | a quarter |
| Screen-space GI resolution (`ssgiResolution`) | screen-space GI | Global Illumination & Occlusion | an eighth |
| Voxel GI gather resolution (`voxelGIResolution`) | the voxel GI gather on WebGL2 | Global Illumination & Occlusion | a quarter |
| Fog volume resolution (`fogResolution`) | the light in height fog | Sky & Volumetrics | an eighth |
| Lens flare resolution (`lensFlareResolution`) | lens flare | Post-Processing | an eighth |

Each is 0.25 at Low, 0.5 at Medium and 1 at High: at Low, ambient occlusion and the voxel gather stop at their quarter and the
others are drawn at an eighth; at Medium all of them at a quarter; at High as authored.

```json
"quality": { "profiles": { "medium": { "aoResolution": 0.25, "fogResolution": 0.25 } } }
```

A game's own settings screen can change each over the level, as it can the render scale: `quality.setPreferences({ fogResolution: 0.5 })`
on the `Quality` resource, and `setPreferences(null)` gives the level's values back.

Where each effect stops, and why there, was judged from screenshots of `third-person-3d`, `terrain-water` and `bistro` at
a phone's size (390 × 844 at a pixel ratio of 2.5):

| Effect | Smallest | What smaller looked like |
|---|---|---|
| Ambient occlusion (SSAO, GTAO) | a quarter | at an eighth the occlusion stains a flat face in blocks and bleeds onto the box that casts it |
| Fog light, screen-space GI, lens flare | an eighth | at a sixteenth the bounced light on `bistro`'s doors shifts to a smooth wash |
| Voxel GI gather (WebGL2) | a quarter | not measured; held where ambient occlusion is |
| Sky clouds | stays at half | at a quarter the clouds' edges streak vertically; no setting takes their size |
| Bloom | stays at half | its first step reads the whole picture with a fixed set of taps, and drawn at a quarter it cost more, not less (0.12 ms against 0.15 on `bistro` at Medium); no setting takes its size |

The occlusion term is laid back over the picture by depth, and how far apart two depths may be and still be the same surface
grows with how much smaller the term is drawn, so a quarter-size term does not band on ground seen at a grazing angle.

Measured in a headless Chromium on the Windows box (RTX 3060, ANGLE on D3D11) at 975 × 2110, GPU time per pass from timer queries, each
level as it was (every effect at resolution 1) against its new defaults. The box's milliseconds are not a phone's; what carries over is the
pixels each pass draws, which fall to a quarter for every halving.

| Scene, level | Passes made smaller | Pixels before → after | Those passes, ms | Whole frame, ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bistro`, Medium | screen-space GI, SSAO and its blurs, fog light | 513,785 → 128,061 each | 4.57 → 1.55 | 11.7 → 8.5 |
| `bistro`, Low | screen-space GI, fog light | 288,715 → 17,927 each | 1.42 → 0.19 | 9.8 → 8.9 |
| `terrain-water`, Medium | fog light, GTAO, lens flare | 513,785 → 128,061 each | 2.26 → 0.84 | 11.7 → 10.4 |
| `terrain-water`, Low | fog light | 288,715 → 17,927 | 0.77 → 0.06 | 6.0 → 5.4 |
| `third-person-3d`, Medium | SSAO and its blurs | 513,785 → 128,061 each | 0.43 → 0.17 | 1.02 → 0.76 |
| any, High | none | unchanged | unchanged | unchanged |

`third-person-3d` at Low runs no ambient occlusion, so nothing changes there. The box shares its GPU with other work, so a single
frame's total moves by a millisecond or more between runs; the pass times are medians.


## Device rules

Shown when the mode is Auto. Each row names a level and the conditions a device must meet: **Model contains**,
**GPU contains**, **Max RAM** and **Max cores**, all of which must hold. The first row that matches decides the level. A
row with no condition would match every device, so it must have one.

## Shader compilation and render on demand

Two groups on the same page hold choices about how a game is *delivered* rather than which level it runs at. They apply
whatever the quality level, **Off** included, and are saved under `features.rendering.quality`.

![Project Settings, Rendering page: the Shader Compilation, Render on Demand, Power Saving and Antialiasing groups](@/assets/guides/en/settings-shader-compilation.png)

**Shader Compilation**

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Compile shaders in the background** (`asyncShaderCompile`) | A draw whose shader the browser is still compiling is left out, so the game opens at once and fills in as shaders are ready. Off, the first draw to need a shader waits for it: every frame is complete, but a first visit can stall for seconds. Needs `KHR_parallel_shader_compile`; without it shaders compile as before. |
| **Warm up behind the loading screen** (`prewarmShaders`) | Draws the first scene out of sight, behind the loading screen, until no shader is compiling, then opens on a complete picture. Shown once background compilation is on. |
| **Warm-up timeout** | The longest the loading screen waits (in seconds, default 30) before opening anyway. Shown once warm-up is on. |

**Render on Demand**

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Draw only changing frames** (`renderOnDemand`) | A still screen costs the game's logic and nothing else. A frame is drawn when something asked for it, the player did something, the surface changed size, entities were created or destroyed, or shaders are still compiling. |
| **Frames kept after a change** (`renderOnDemandTail`) | Frames still drawn after the last reason to draw, so a fade or a settling motion finishes. Default 30. |

The engine cannot see what a script changes by itself (a colour, an animation). Say so with `RenderDemand.request()` for
one frame, or `RenderDemand.hold()` (it returns the function that releases it) for something that moves on its own, or
that change is not drawn until something else wakes the screen. That is why the switch is opt-in.

## Saving power

A long session on a phone is heat and battery, and the frame is where both go. Turn on **Save power when the battery is low or the device is hot** (`QualityConfig.power`) and the engine
watches the device every few seconds: while it is saving it lowers the frame cap and puts a ceiling on the quality level, and when the device recovers it puts both back. Off (the default), the device
is not watched and nothing changes.

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Battery below** (`lowBattery`) | Saves while the battery is under this share and not charging. Default 20%; 0 never saves for the battery. |
| **Thermal pressure** (`thermal`) | Saves when the platform reports pressure of this level or worse: Serious (default), Critical, or Never. |
| **Frame cap while saving** (`fps`) | Frames a second while saving. Default 30. A cap the game already set lower is kept. |
| **Level ceiling while saving** (`ceiling`) | No level above this while saving; a level already under it is left. Default Medium. |

At Critical thermal pressure it goes further: the cap is halved (never under 15) and the level is held at Low (`severe: 'never'` turns that off). What a platform does not report never triggers
anything: a browser with no battery API saves nothing for the battery, and most browsers report no heat at all (Chromium reports the battery and the processor's pressure; the WeChat mini-game
platform reports the battery). `Device.power()` gives a game the same numbers (`level`, `charging`, `thermal`, each `null` where the platform does not say), and `Quality`'s report names `power-saver`
among its limitations while it is holding the level down. The cap is put back only if the game has not changed it itself since.

For a still screen (a menu, a card table) the better saving is to draw nothing: see render-on-demand in the same page of the editor's settings.

## In code

The same settings are `QualityConfig` — `QualityController`, `parseQualityConfig`, `resolveQualityProfile` and
`defaultQualityProfile` build and read them — and the running level is the `Quality` resource.

Every per-level setting is one entry of `QUALITY_SETTINGS`, declared in the file of its group under
`sdk/src/render/qualitySettings/`. An entry gives its key, its kind (a number with a range, a switch, a choice of numbers or a
choice of names), its three defaults, its group, and its label and description in English and Chinese; the profile's type, its
defaults, its parsing, a player preference where the entry allows one and the editor's row are all made from it.
