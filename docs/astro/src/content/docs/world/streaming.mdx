World streaming cuts an authored world into square cells at cook time. At runtime a cell exists while a streaming
source is near it and is destroyed when none is. A cell that unloads is not hidden: its entities, physics bodies,
navigating agents and asset references are all released, so an unloaded place costs no memory, does not collide, and
runs no logic.

A scene without a `StreamedWorld` is not a streamed world and loads whole.

## Declaring a streamed world

Two components declare a streamed world, both authored in the scene:

```typescript
// On the world's settings entity: this scene is cut into 1000-unit cells.
world.insert(settings, StreamedWorld, { cellSize: 1000 });

// On everything that must never be streamed away.
world.insert(player, WorldPersistent, {});
world.insert(camera, WorldPersistent, {});
world.insert(sun, WorldPersistent, {});
```

Every other entity is placed into a cell by the position of its top-level entity. `WorldPersistent` keeps the entity's
whole subtree in the persistent world, which is never streamed. The `StreamedWorld` entity is persistent by
construction.

### `StreamedWorld` fields

| Field | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `cellSize` | `1000` | Edge of one square cell, in world units, on the plane below. |
| `plane` | `Ground` | `StreamPlane.Ground` cuts cells along world x and z (a 3D world); `StreamPlane.Screen` cuts along world x and y (a 2D world). See [Cell plane](#cell-plane). |
| `farProxies` | `false` | Bake a far proxy for each cell at build time ([Far proxies](#far-proxies)). |
| `farDistance` | `8000` | Distance from a source within which a non-resident cell shows its far proxy. |
| `proxyError` | `1` | Largest distance, in world units, a far-proxy surface may move from the original. |
| `proxyRatio` | `0.01` | Fewest triangles a far proxy aims for, as a share of its cell's, in (0, 1]. `proxyError` keeps more where required. |

In the editor, **World Settings → Enable Streaming** adds the `StreamedWorld` component to the world settings entity.

<Aside type="note">
`WorldPersistent` is not scene persistence. `SceneContext.setPersistent` decides whether an entity survives a scene
switch; `WorldPersistent` decides which cooked scene an entity is written into, and therefore whether residency can
remove it.
</Aside>

## Streaming sources

A cell exists while a `WorldStreamingSource` is near it:

```typescript
world.insert(player, WorldStreamingSource, {
    prefetchRadius: 3000, // cells this close are READIED, not brought in
    loadRadius: 2000,     // cells this close are brought in
    unloadRadius: 3000,   // resident cells are kept until past this
});
```

| Field | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `prefetchRadius` | `3000` | Cells whose nearest edge is within this distance are prepared but not published. Never used as less than `loadRadius`. |
| `loadRadius` | `2000` | Cells whose nearest edge is within this distance are brought into the world. |
| `unloadRadius` | `3000` | A resident cell is released once its nearest edge is beyond this distance. Must exceed `loadRadius`. |
| `enabled` | `true` | `false` removes the source from the union without removing the component. |

Radii are in world units. Several sources may exist; the cells they request are **unioned**, and a cell any source
needs stays resident. Split screen, a spectator camera, a cinematic camera and an editor preview are all sources of the
same kind. Distance is measured to the cell's bounding box, not its centre, so a source near a cell's corner is treated
as near that cell.

### Prefetch

A cell inside `prefetchRadius` is **prepared**: its document is fetched, its prefabs expanded and its assets acquired,
but none of its entities exist. No query returns it, no renderer draws it and no body collides with it. Publication
creates the entities, and it happens only when a source reaches `loadRadius`. On the engine's test fixture, publishing
a prepared cell took 0.6 ms; an unprepared cell also pays for fetching and decoding.

Preparation is speculative. A cell prepared for a source that turns away is discarded and its assets released; it is
never published because it happens to be ready. When a preparation completes, the streamer checks again whether the
cell is still wanted.

`prefetchRadius` and `unloadRadius` are independent: one sets how early a cell is prepared, the other how late a
resident cell is released.

### Load and unload band

A cell is published at `loadRadius` and released only beyond `unloadRadius`. The band between them prevents a source on
a cell boundary from loading and unloading the cell every frame. A starting point is an `unloadRadius` 1.5 times
`loadRadius`.

### Publication budget

A cell is published over several frames. Its entities are spawned, their components written and the hierarchy linked
in pieces, within the per-frame budget shared with bakes, terrain pages and procedural chunks (`Bakes.budgetMs`,
8 ms). While a cell arrives its roots are `Disabled`, so no system, query, body or draw sees a partial cell; the last
step enables it whole, and every `Added` filter sees its components in that frame. Unloading is the reverse: the cell
is disabled in the frame it is released, destroyed within the budget, and its assets released afterwards.

A cell's **preparation** is also split into turns (`Bakes.turn`). Fetching the document and assets happens off the
frame; the work that runs when they arrive (parsing the document, expanding prefabs, resolving assets, readying shader
programs) waits for a turn. Each frame grants as many turns as its remaining budget fits, and at least one. A cell that
is urgent, or that is rushed because the world is being cleared, takes no turns. Decoding an asset (a mesh, a texture)
is not split.

When a world is cleared (`clear()`, Play stopped), cells in progress are finished and removed at once.

### Streaming policy

The streamer's concurrency and its behaviour when it falls behind are its **policy**, set with
`streamer.setPolicy({...})`. The frame budget itself belongs to the `Bakes` resource, shared by all work spread over
frames.

| Setting | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Bakes.budgetMs` | 8 | Milliseconds per frame for all work spread over frames. |
| `Bakes.priorityExtraMs` | 12 | Additional milliseconds per frame while urgent work (such as a cell a source is standing in) is still arriving. |
| `maxLoadingCells` | 4 | Cells prepared at once; the rest wait, nearest first. An urgent cell never waits. |
| `maxPublishingCells` | 1 | Cells published at once. An urgent cell never waits. |
| `slowStreamingRatio` | 0.25 | A wanted cell is **urgent** while a source is within this share of its `loadRadius` of it. |
| `slowStreaming` | `'wait'` | What an urgent cell does (below). |

`slowStreaming` options:

| Value | Behaviour | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| `'wait'` | An urgent cell is finished in the frame it becomes urgent, and the game is **held** (paused, with streaming continuing) until every urgent cell is in. | A hitch and a pause; never a hole. |
| `'publish'` | An urgent cell is finished at once; the game is never held. | A cell still being fetched is missing until it arrives. |
| `'budget'` | Nothing runs outside the budget; an urgent cell only receives `priorityExtraMs`. | The steadiest frame rate; a hole when streaming cannot keep up. |

A hold is a pause owned by the streamer. A pause requested by the game (a menu) stops streaming as before, and a hold
never ends that pause. A cell that failed to prepare or publish does not hold the game.

`maxLoadingCells` caps concurrent preparation; above it, the nearest cells are prepared first. A higher value brings in
the far side of a fast-moving view sooner and gives the turns more work to share.

## Cook output

Partitioning happens at cook time:

```
main.esscene ──cook──▶ main.esscene          the persistent world
                       world/main.world.json the manifest
                       world/main.cell_0_0.json
                       world/main.cell_1_0.json ...
```

Each streamed scene keeps its persistent entities in its own scene document. Game code names the map, and residency
loads its cells.

- In editor Play and in Web and native packages, `SceneManager.switchTo(name)` releases the old map's cells and far
  proxies before the new map begins streaming. Switching to a menu releases them too.
- `loadAdditive` adds a scene (such as a HUD) while the active map keeps streaming.
- `load(name)` makes an already loaded map active. Streaming sources belonging to that map start contributing when it
  becomes active.
- `WorldMemory.toJSON()` saves the current world's remembered entities; returning to a previous world restores its own
  memory ([Remembered state](#remembered-state)).

### Cell plane

Cells are cut on one plane. By default (`StreamPlane.Ground`) the plane is the ground, world x and z: a cell is a
vertical column, so a tower's floors are one place. A 2D world lies in the screen plane, where every entity has the
same z; set `plane` to **Screen (x, y)** (`StreamPlane.Screen`) to cut cells along world x and y, and streaming sources
are then read by their x and y. Without it a 2D world falls into a single row of cells along x. The cooked manifest
carries the same choice as `WorldManifest.plane: 'xy'`, and a cell's `z` is then world y.

## Far proxies

A non-resident cell is not in the world, so by default nothing beyond the load radius is drawn. With **Far proxies**
on `StreamedWorld`, a build bakes each cell a stand-in: the cell's static, opaque meshes merged into one draw per
material and simplified, shown while the cell is absent.

```typescript
world.insert(settings, StreamedWorld, {
    cellSize: 1000,
    farProxies: true,
    farDistance: 8000,   // a cell this close to a source and not resident shows its proxy
    proxyError: 1,       // how far, in world units, a simplified surface may move
    proxyRatio: 0.01,    // the fewest triangles to aim for; proxyError keeps more wherever it must
});
```

- The cook writes `world/<cell>.far.json` and its meshes beside the cell and names them in the manifest
  (`WorldCell.proxy`, with `WorldManifest.farDistance`).
- Prefab instances are expanded, so a building placed as a prefab contributes its meshes.
- A surface is excluded when it is disabled, blended, skinned or a billboard, or when it or a parent moves on its own (a
  character controller, a non-static body, an animator). A mesh moved by game code without any of those would be baked
  where it stands: put `ExcludeFromFarProxy` on it, or on an entity above it, to keep that subtree out of the proxy. The
  subtree is still drawn whenever its cell is resident.
- `proxyError` determines how far a cell simplifies. Keep it below the size of a pixel at the distance where the proxy
  starts (beyond the sources' load radius).
- Surfaces that differ only in colour share one draw, with the colour carried in the vertices. A proxy carries no
  lightmap UV.

Handover is gapless in both directions: approaching a cell, its proxy remains until the cell is resident; leaving, the
cell remains until its proxy is loaded. A proxy that fails to load does not hold its cell.
`WorldStreamerStatus.proxyCells` and the residency report list the cells currently drawn by their proxy.

Play builds the same proxies from the same cells with the same simplification code as a build, and keeps them in
memory. Each proxy is built the first time the streamer requests it.

Measured in the `far-proxy-town` example (144 cells, 3.1 million triangles of static meshes, `proxyError` 0.5):

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Triangles kept by the proxies | 2% |
| A frame along the example's flight path | 122 thousand triangles in 354 draws |
| The town fully loaded | 866 thousand triangles in 620 draws |
| Pixels noticeably different | 0.3% |
| Play start (naming the cells that have a proxy) | 172 ms |
| Building one proxy in Play | about 30 ms, at most 190 ms |

The editor's **World** panel has an **HLOD** view, beside **Map** and **List**, that lists each cell's proxy with its
original and reduced triangle counts and its build state.

## Rules for streamed content

**A subtree is the unit of residency.** Only top-level entities are placed, so a door is never separated from its house
even if the door's world position falls in the next cell. A cell's bounding box grows to cover its content, so a prop
overhanging the edge does not pop in at the boundary.

**Hard entity references may not cross a residency boundary.** Two entities in one cell may reference each other, and
so may entities in the persistent world; a cell may reference the persistent world, which outlives it. A reference
from one cell into another, or from the persistent world into a cell, fails the build, because it points at an entity
residency may delete.

**Residency owns existence; continuity is opt-in.** A cell that unloads loses its runtime state, and reloading rebuilds
it from the authored content: a wounded enemy returns at full health.

### Remembered state

`RememberState` keeps an entity as it was left when its cell unloads:

```typescript
world.insert(enemy, RememberState, {});                     // its health and where it stood
world.insert(chest, RememberState, { keepTransform: false }); // opened stays opened
```

- When a remembered entity is unloaded with its cell, its game components (those the project defines, such as
  `Health` or a chest's `opened` flag) and its `Transform` are recorded under its authored id. `keepTransform: false`
  leaves the `Transform` out.
- When the cell loads again, its document is modified before anything spawns, so the entity arrives as it was left and
  never appears in its authored state for a frame.
- A remembered entity destroyed while its cell is resident (a boss killed, an ore mined, a building torn down) is
  recorded as gone, with its subtree, and the cell does not bring it back.
- Engine components are not kept: they hold asset handles and state the cell rebuilds.
- Entities spawned at run time have no authored id and are not remembered.

The memory is the `WorldMemoryRes` resource. Carry it through a save with [save slots](/docs/scripting/save/):

```typescript
const saves = new SaveManager({ version: 1 });
const memory = app.getResource(WorldMemoryRes);
saves.save('slot1', { player, world: memory.toJSON() });   // when saving
const loaded = saves.load<{ world: WorldMemoryData }>('slot1');
if (loaded) memory.read(loaded.world);                     // when loading, before the world streams in
```

`read` replaces the memory of the world named in the save; each cell reads it the next time it loads. `clear()` clears
the memory of all visited worlds, for a new game.

## Procedural chunks

Content a game generates at runtime (ground collision around moving bodies, plants around the view, a game's own
generated content) is not a cooked cell but a chunk of a **layer**. `ProceduralChunks` owns the lifetime of chunks as
the streamer owns cells. A layer defines how it is cut, how far around its sources it is wanted, and how one chunk is
made, published and released:

```typescript
import { chunksOf } from 'esengine';

chunksOf(app).set('ruins', {
    label: 'Placing ruins',
    originX: 0, originZ: 0, sizeX: 64, sizeZ: 64, extent: null,
    loadRadius: 256, unloadRadius: 320,
    generation: `${planet}:${generatorVersion}`,     // what the chunks are made from
    make: (cx, cz, signal) => generateRuins(cx, cz, signal), // a value, null for "not yet", or a promise
    publish: (cx, cz, ruins) => spawnRuins(ruins),
    release: (cx, cz, ruins) => despawnRuins(ruins),
});
```

- **Sources.** A layer is wanted around the same `WorldStreamingSource`s the streamer reads (or the view, where there
  are none), unless it supplies its own `sources()`. Terrain collision, for example, follows every moving 3D body and
  character.
- **Plane.** A layer is cut on the ground (world x and z) by default. With `plane: 'xy'` it is cut along world x and y
  and its sources are read the same way; every "z" the layer names (`originZ`, `sizeZ`, the chunk's `cz`, the second
  coordinate of `stateAt`) is then world y. A layer's own `sources()` already answer in the layer's plane.
- **Budget.** Each frame the wanted chunks of every layer are made nearest first: the chunks a source stands in
  immediately, the rest while the frame's budget lasts. The budget is `Bakes.budgetMs`, shared with bakes and terrain
  pages.
- **Bounds.** A chunk beyond every source's `unloadRadius` is released with everything it published, so the number of
  live chunks stays bounded; moving back and forth inside the band does not remake chunks.
- **Generations.** `generation` identifies a layer's inputs. When it changes every chunk is remade, and its old version
  stays published until the new one replaces it, so nothing standing on it falls. A result that arrives for an older
  generation is discarded and the chunk's `AbortSignal` is aborted.
- **Status.** `status(id)` counts a layer's chunks (`ready`, `stale`, `pending`, `making`, `failed`, with the first
  failure's reason), and `stateAt(id, x, z)` reports the state of the chunk at a point. Layers still making chunks
  appear with their progress in the editor's status bar, alongside the bakes.

## Residency report

```typescript
const report = worldResidencyReport(app);
report.residentCells;        // ['main.cell_1_0', 'main.cell_2_0']
report.cellEntityCounts;     // live entities each resident cell owns
report.loadCount;            // places brought in since boot
```

The report gives counts rather than an image, because a cell that is not drawn and a cell that does not exist look the
same from a camera. Besides the cell lists (`desiredCells`, `prefetchCells`, `residentCells`, `preparedCells`,
`loadingCells`, `unloadingCells`, `proxyCells`), it reports the policy's activity: `urgentCells`, `queuedCells`,
`holding`, `holdCount`, `holdMs` and `rushCount` (publications finished outside the budget).

## In the editor

In the edit view, terrain detail, foliage, generated worlds and voxels are generated around the viewport, in 2D and 3D;
a `WorldStreamingSource` in the scene takes effect only while the game runs.

A scene with `StreamedWorld` opens with only its `WorldPersistent` entities in the view, and the textures and meshes of
its cells are not loaded. The **World** panel shows the cells as a map or a list and loads and unloads them one at a time
or all at once; the viewport marks the loaded cells, and the next session opens with the same cells loaded. During Play
the panel shows each cell's runtime state (Resident, Prepared, Loading, Unloading, Unloaded). The scene document always
holds every entity: an unloaded cell is saved unchanged, and Play and export see the whole world.

## Clearing or replacing a world

Disable or remove the streaming sources, then await `streamer.clear()`. It releases prepared assets and waits for
pending preparation, publication and unloading to finish. Registrations remain reusable: a later source update can
request the cells again.

Await `streamer.loadManifest(manifest, sceneConfig)` to replace a world. It drains the old cells before registering the
replacement, including when both manifests use the same cell names. The latest concurrent replacement request wins; an
explicit `clear()` cancels pending replacement requests. A failed teardown rejects the operation, so the replacement
cannot take ownership of a name that still has live content. `await streamer.loadManifest(null)` detaches the world and
releases its cell and proxy registrations.

## Limitations

- **One component write is not split.** A single entity whose components alone exceed the budget still costs its frame.
- **Far proxies carry static meshes only.** What moves, blends or is not a mesh (sprites, particles, lights) is not drawn
  while its cell is absent, and a proxy has one level, not a chain of coarser levels.
- **Content with its own far representation:** the ground ([Terrain](/docs/graphics/terrain/overview/) is a
  distance-based quadtree out to `drawDistance`; tiled ground draws distant terrain from an overview that is always in
  memory and reads full-detail tiles only near the view; a planet is drawn to its horizon), and plants (the far cards of
  [Foliage](/docs/graphics/foliage/) and `PlacedFoliage`, and of a generated world's plants), which cover a whole area
  at once. Mesh textures are kept at the size their views need ([texture streaming](/docs/performance/quality-levels/),
  a quality setting).
- **Navigation** is baked one tile per cell square and follows residency (see
  [Navigation](/docs/gameplay/ai/navigation/#in-a-streamed-world)), but agents walk only where the world is loaded.
- **The world origin is never rebased.** `setRenderOriginStep` keeps drawing precise far from zero (see
  [Camera](/docs/graphics/camera/multiple-cameras/#large-worlds)); simulation precision is measured below.

### Precision far from the origin

Positions, physics and navigation use 32-bit floats, so the smallest distinguishable position step grows with distance
from the origin. Measured in a package (`node tools/measure-world-precision.mjs`) at the default 3D scale of 100 units to
a metre, with the probe at each distance along x and z:

| From the origin | A child under a moving parent, largest error | A body pushed at 300 units/s (5 a frame), largest step error | A navmesh route round a wall |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.00002 units | 0.0001 | 1216.06, the reference |
| 1 km | 0.008 units (0.08 mm) | 0.008 | identical |
| 10 km | 0.06 units (0.6 mm) | 0.06 | identical |
| 50 km | 0.5 units (5 mm) | 0.5 | identical |
| 100 km | 1 unit (1 cm) | 1: it moves 4 or 6 a frame, not 5 | identical |
| 500 km | 4 units (4 cm) | 3: 2 to 8 a frame | its corners move by 2 units |

Within about **10 km** of the origin no error exceeds a millimetre. Beyond about **50 km**, slow movement advances in
visible steps: a body moving less per frame than the position step there cannot move smoothly (at 100 km a walk of a few
centimetres a frame jitters by about a third). Keep a world's playable area within about 50 km of the origin; the engine
does not move the origin to follow a player who goes further.
